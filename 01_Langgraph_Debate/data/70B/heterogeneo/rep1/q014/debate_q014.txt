[DLB] When optimizing antenna aperture size for CMOS-based ground terminals to balance gain and portability between LEO and GEO satellites, several factors come into play:

1. **LEO Satellites**: 
   - Due to their closer proximity to Earth, LEO satellites experience less free space path loss (FSPL). This allows for smaller aperture sizes while still achieving adequate gain, enhancing portability.
   - The reduced signal propagation delay in LEO systems supports real-time applications without compromising antenna design.

2. **GEO Satellites**:
   - Being farther from Earth, GEO satellites encounter higher FSPL, necessitating larger antennas to maintain sufficient gain. This can limit portability as bigger antennas are less convenient to transport and deploy.
   - The larger footprint of GEO beams may allow for smaller ground terminal apertures, but this is offset by the need for higher gain to counteract path loss.

3. **Beam Size Considerations**:
   - LEO systems benefit from smaller beam sizes due to their lower altitude, which can be managed with compact antennas.
   - GEO systems typically have larger beams, potentially reducing the required aperture size per terminal but still needing overall larger antennas for sufficient gain.

In balancing these factors, LEO terminals can favor portability with smaller apertures, while GEO may require larger antennas to ensure performance, impacting deployment flexibility. This trade-off hinges on the specific requirements of mobility versus signal strength in each application context.
