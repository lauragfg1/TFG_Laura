------------------------------
[DLB] The use of additive manufacturing (AM) in fabricating CTS arrays introduces considerations regarding the coefficients of thermal expansion (CTE). AM materials, such as photopolymer resins used in stereolithography, typically have higher CTEs compared to traditional metals like aluminum or copper. This discrepancy can lead to mechanical stress and potential failure due to differential expansion under temperature fluctuations, especially in space environments where temperatures vary significantly.

In large constellations, this mismatch could result in structural deformation or separation, impacting signal integrity and performance. While AM offers cost savings upfront, the higher CTE of its materials may lead to long-term reliability issues, necessitating careful material selection to mitigate these effects and ensure consistent performance across the constellation.
