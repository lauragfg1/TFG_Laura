[DLB] The trade-offs between array size and radiation efficiency in SIW arrays for high-gain inter-satellite antennas involve several key considerations:

1. **Insertion Loss and Efficiency**: As array size increases, longer transmission lines in SIW structures lead to higher insertion losses. At 60 GHz, a loss of 0.034 dB/mm significantly impacts efficiency, dropping it from 70% to 27.5% when scaling from 16 to 32 elements.

2. **Feeding Topology Optimization**: Using hybrid SIW waveguide power dividers can mitigate performance degradation while maintaining compactness, balancing the need for high gain with minimal loss.

3. **Physical Mechanisms**: Larger arrays offer higher directivity but may suffer from interference issues like grating lobes if element spacing isn't optimized. Accurate material characterization is crucial to predict losses and maintain efficiency.

4. **Design Considerations**: Experimental validation of different array sizes helps identify optimal configurations. Mixing technologies, such as hybrid integrated plate arrays, can enhance performance without compromising size.

5. **Application Context**: High gain is essential for inter-satellite communication, but low efficiency due to insertion losses can degrade system performance in terms of data rate and power consumption.

In conclusion, balancing these factors requires optimizing feeding structures, accurate modeling, and careful design to achieve high gain while maintaining efficient radiation performance.
