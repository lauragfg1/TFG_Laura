[DLB] **Optimizing Aperture Size for LEO vs. GEO Satellites: A Performance Balance**

When optimizing aperture size for CMOS-based ground terminals in LEO and GEO satellite systems, several trade-offs emerge:

1. **Gain vs. Portability**: 
   - Larger apertures increase gain but reduce portability. For LEO satellites, which are closer to Earth, smaller apertures can achieve similar gain as larger ones used for GEO due to lower free space path loss (FSPL). This allows for more portable terminals without significant performance loss.

2. **Beamwidth and Interference**:
   - Smaller apertures result in wider beamwidths, potentially increasing interference. LEO systems, with their moving beams, require careful aperture design to maintain directivity and minimize interference from adjacent satellites or ground sources.

3. **Propagation Characteristics**:
   - LEO's lower propagation delay allows for real-time communication with less signal degradation, supporting smaller apertures effectively. In contrast, GEO's fixed position requires larger apertures to compensate for higher FSPL and longer delays, sacrificing portability for performance.

4. **System Constraints**:
   - LEO backhaul capacity is constrained by ground-to-satellite links, which have lower bandwidth and transmit power. Optimizing aperture size must consider these constraints to maintain link integrity without oversizing the antenna.

5. **Array Design Considerations**:
   - CMOS-based terminals benefit from array designs that balance directivity and portability. While larger arrays enhance gain and reduce interference, they may compromise the compact form factor needed for mobile applications.

In conclusion, aperture size optimization for LEO focuses on leveraging proximity to Earth for smaller, portable designs without sacrificing performance, while GEO systems prioritize higher gain at the expense of portability
