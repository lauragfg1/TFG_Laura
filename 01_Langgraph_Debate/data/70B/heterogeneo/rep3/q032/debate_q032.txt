[DLB] The optimal feed element spacing balance in defocused systems for minimizing dB loss while maintaining beamforming flexibility involves considering array factor physics, interference effects, and robustness against failures. 

1. **Array Factor and Interference**: 
   - Feed element spacing affects the array's ability to form sharp beams and minimize sidelobes. Closer spacing can lead to narrower beams but increases mutual coupling, causing higher dB loss. Wider spacing reduces mutual coupling but may broaden beamwidths.

2. **Robustness Against Failures**:
   - Defocused reflectors are more robust against feed element failures. Wider spacing helps maintain performance when elements fail by reducing the impact on overall gain and beamforming capability.

3. **Beamforming Flexibility and Throughput**:
   - The number of beams and throughput per beam (2-5 Gb/s) require a balance where spacing allows sufficient elements for multiple beams without excessive mutual interference, ensuring each beam maintains necessary SNR for performance.

4. **Optimization Strategy**:
   - Calculate optimal spacing to minimize dB loss from mutual coupling while preserving enough elements for required beamforming flexibility and throughput. This balance ensures robustness against failures and maintains high beam gain (around 51.8 dBi).

In summary, the optimal feed element spacing balances array factor considerations, interference mitigation, and failure robustness to achieve minimal dB loss while maintaining beamforming capability in defocused systems.
