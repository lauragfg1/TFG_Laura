[DLB] The coordination of multiple ground stations with overlapping satellite visibility windows presents several physical mechanisms that impact frequency planning:

1. **Array Factor**: Each ground station's antenna array has a radiation pattern that can cause unintended interference with other stations if not aligned correctly. Proper alignment is crucial to minimize signal overlap.

2. **Interference**: Operating at 2.2GHz (S band) across different environments increases the likelihood of interference, especially when multiple stations transmit simultaneously within the same satellite's visibility window. This leads to a higher noise floor and degraded signal quality.

3. **Spacing Effects**: The physical distance between ground stations affects interference levels. Inadequate spacing can result in significant signal overlap, exacerbating interference issues.

**Budget Implications**:

- **Performance Degradation**: Increased interference leads to lower throughput and higher error rates, necessitating retransmissions and reducing overall efficiency.
  
- **Capacity Reduction**: Interference mitigation efforts may strain advanced scheduling techniques like Load Balancing and Perceptive Scheduling, diminishing their effectiveness.

- **Spectral Efficiency**: Without proper frequency planning, even with technologies like cognitive radio (CR) and Software Defined Radios (SDRs), spectral efficiency could be compromised due to suboptimal integration into the overall strategy.

In conclusion, addressing these physical mechanisms through careful frequency planning is essential to mitigate performance degradation, capacity reduction, and inefficiencies in spectral usage.
