[DLB] Distributed satellite constellations face trade-offs between coverage and interference mitigation due to the physical mechanisms of array factors and spacing effects. As satellites increase, interference rises because signals overlap more. Strategies include optimizing orbital altitudes and phasing to minimize overlaps, using directional antennas to focus signals and reduce unintended receptions, and employing time-division or code-division multiple access protocols to prevent simultaneous transmissions in the same band. These choices impact the link budget by reducing interference power at the cost of increased system complexity and resource allocation constraints.

LDMA protocols balance channel utilization but may struggle with scalability due to complex time scheduling. Hybrid TDMA/CDMA offers high throughput and low delay, suitable for scalable missions, though strict synchronization is required. For inter-plane ISLs, orthogonal resource allocation can mitigate interference by preventing simultaneous transmission on the same frequencies between satellites in different planes. This approach enhances network capacity and reliability while maintaining comprehensive coverage.

Additionally, coordinating beams across numerous satellites to avoid interference is challenging. While beamforming reduces interference, it may leave some areas with less coverage unless overlapping beams are carefully managed. Thus, beamforming optimizes signal power use but demands advanced system management to balance coverage and interference effectively.

This trade-off between enhanced coverage and increased system complexity is critical in optimizing constellation design for both performance and scalability.
