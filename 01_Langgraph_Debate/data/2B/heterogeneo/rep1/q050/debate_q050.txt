[PNM] **Beamforming Strategies and Dynamic Spectrum Sharing in LEO A2A Networks**

In LEO (Low Earth Orbit) Air-to-Air (A2A) networks, maintaining consistent Line-of-Sight (LOS) connectivity between aircraft is challenging due to the high velocity and dynamic nature of flying objects. Adaptive beamforming techniques are essential for this stability, as they dynamically adjust signal strength and direction based on AoA (Angle of Arrival), accounting for varying velocities and altitudes. This ensures that each aircraft receives a dedicated communication path without overlapping coverage areas, mitigating interference from nearby aircraft.

Dynamic spectrum sharing complements beamforming by allowing multiple A2A links to operate on shared frequency bands, reducing interference and enhancing network efficiency. Techniques like Coordinated Multi-Point (CoMP) and Direct-Sequence Spread Spectrum (DSSS) are employed to optimize signal distribution across different frequencies, ensuring reliable communication while avoiding clutter.

In summary, adaptive beamforming strategies ensure stability by adjusting signals dynamically, while dynamic spectrum sharing optimizes resource utilization, leading to more efficient and stable A2A connectivity in LEO networks.
