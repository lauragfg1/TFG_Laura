[DLB] In heterogeneous satellite networks, antenna design plays a crucial role in determining the accuracy of encrypted traffic classification systems. The choice of antennas significantly impacts signal propagation patterns and interference levels, which affect classifier performance. 

### Antenna Types:
- **Individual Antennas:** Low complexity but high budget due to limited diversity.
- **Antenna Arrays:** High complexity with low budget because they offer enhanced diversity for better channel estimation.

### Signal Propagation Patterns:
- **Interference Levels:** Higher interference can degrade classification accuracy by reducing signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).
- **Spacing Effects:** Antennas spaced too closely can cause overlapping coverage, leading to potential misclassification of encrypted traffic.

### Budget Implications:
- **Individual Antennas:**
  - Lower complexity means reduced hardware costs but higher maintenance and calibration requirements.
  - Higher budget due to the need for multiple antennas with different polarizations and gain patterns.

- **Antenna Arrays:**
  - High complexity necessitates more sophisticated algorithms and potentially larger computational resources, increasing software development and implementation costs.
  - Lower budget as arrays can be designed to balance performance and cost effectively by optimizing antenna spacing and diversity.

### Conclusion:
To improve classification accuracy in heterogeneous satellite networks, designers should consider the trade-offs between antenna type (individual vs. array) and their impact on signal propagation patterns and interference levels. By leveraging antenna arrays with optimized spacing, systems can achieve better classification performance without significantly increasing costs beyond what is necessary for high diversity.
