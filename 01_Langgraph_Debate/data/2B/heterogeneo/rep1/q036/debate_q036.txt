[DLB] In adverse weather conditions like rain, fog, or high atmospheric turbulence, signal propagation through satellite links is significantly impacted by Doppler effects and frequency drifts due to moving objects (like clouds). This leads to reduced transmit power and increased latency.

**Impact of Atmospheric Turbulence:**
- **Doppler Effects:** As the Earth's atmosphere moves relative to satellites in orbit, signals undergo frequency shifts (Doppler effect), leading to errors in carrier phase measurements. These errors can cause incorrect calculations of signal strength and distance.
- **Frequency Drifts:** Higher turbulence levels result in more frequent Doppler shifts, causing higher-frequency drifts that further degrade the accuracy of link parameters like path loss and delay.

**Mitigation Strategies:**
1. **Modulation Schemes:** Higher modulation orders (like 64QAM) can tolerate lower transmit powers better by spreading power across multiple bits.
2. **Antenna Design:** Antennas with higher gain and narrower beamwidths can mitigate signal degradation from atmospheric effects, ensuring reliable communication despite adverse weather.
3. **Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR):** Higher transmit powers are necessary to overcome increased noise due to Doppler shifts, requiring more robust error correction schemes or enhanced antenna designs.

By understanding these physical mechanisms and optimizing modulation schemes and antenna design, we can balance data rate performance with reliability under challenging atmospheric conditions, ensuring high-QoS satellite backhaul links.
