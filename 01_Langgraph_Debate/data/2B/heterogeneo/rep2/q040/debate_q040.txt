[DLB] ### Tradeoffs between SIW and Waveguide for Hybrid Feeding Topology

#### Advantages of SIW:
- **High Efficiency at Smaller Scales**: SIW has a higher efficiency when used in smaller arrays, which is beneficial for achieving high gain or minimizing insertion loss in the early stages of array development.
- **Compact Design**: SIW can be designed to have more efficient radiation patterns with smaller apertures due to its inherent characteristics. This makes it suitable for compact antenna designs.

#### Advantages of Waveguides:
- **Broad Bandwidth Capability**: As array size increases, waveguides provide a broader bandwidth capability compared to SIW, which is crucial for achieving high gain and minimizing insertion loss in the later stages of array design.
- **Reduced Insertion Loss**: Waveguide feeding topology can result in lower insertion losses due to their inherent characteristics, making them more suitable for large arrays.

#### Balancing Compactness and Efficiency:
The key challenge lies in optimizing a hybrid SIW-waveguide feeding topology that achieves high gain or low insertion loss while keeping the array compact. This involves carefully designing the transition between SiW and waveguides to minimize interference effects and ensure efficient power distribution across the array.

### Experimental Comparison
- **Radiation Efficiency**: By comparing radiation efficiency of SiW arrays with different sizes experimentally, it was found that larger apertures in SiW arrays lead to higher insertion losses. This highlights the importance of optimizing the transition between SiW and waveguides.
- **Insertion Loss**: The additional length of 102.4 mm produced a significant increase in insertion loss (3.5 dB), emphasizing the need for careful design choices.

### Conclusion
The hybrid SIW-waveguide feeding topology balances efficiency and compactness by leveraging the strengths of both SiW and waveguides. Optimizing this transition ensures high gain, low insertion loss, and a compact configuration while meeting the performance requirements of V-band satellite antenna designs.
