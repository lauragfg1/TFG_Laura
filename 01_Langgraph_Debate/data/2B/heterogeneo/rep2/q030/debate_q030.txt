[DLB] In DBF architectures, defocused feed array placement optimizes coverage by reducing scan loss while maintaining optimal performance. The key mechanisms include:

- **Array Factor**: Defocus increases the gain at specific angles (e.g., near edges) compared to conventional arrays, enhancing coverage in those areas.

- **Interference and Spacing Effects**: By focusing on a subset of elements for enhanced coverage, defocused feed placement minimizes interference with other antennas. The reduced scanning required due to defocusing also reduces overall scan loss.

Key considerations for implementing defocused arrays include:
1. **Element Selection**: Focus on the outermost or peripheral elements for enhanced coverage.
2. **Gain and Loss Minimization**: Balance gain reduction in high-scanning areas against minimal spillover into neighboring cells, ensuring optimal performance across the desired coverage area.
3. **Feed Array Geometry**: Optimize spacing between feed elements to minimize loss from non-uniform excitation patterns.

Compared to conventional approaches:
- Defocused arrays typically offer slightly reduced overall gain due to scan losses but significantly lower spillover and taper losses, making them more robust against individual element failures.
- Conventional reflector antennas, without redundancy or margin planning, would suffer severely in the presence of feed failures, losing up to 10 dB across all directions.

In summary, defocused feed placement strikes a balance by reducing scan loss while maintaining high coverage efficiency, addressing the trade-off between efficient signal reception and optimal network performance.
