[DLB] **Trade-offs: Additive Manufacturing vs Traditional Methods for CTS Array Fabrication**

**Deployment Cost:**
- **Additive Manufacturing (AM):** Higher initial cost due to specialized equipment, materials, and labor.
- **Traditional Methods:** Lower upfront costs but higher ongoing maintenance and material usage.

**Scalability:**
- **AM:** Limited scalability; requires significant investment in new equipment for larger arrays.
- **Traditional Methods:** More scalable with existing infrastructure, allowing gradual expansion without major investments.

**Performance Characteristics:**
- **Additive Manufacturing (AM):** Higher cost per unit due to higher material and labor costs. Lower performance compared to traditional methods.
- **Traditional Methods:** Lower initial investment but lower per-unit cost. Better performance in terms of bandwidth, scanning range, and impedance matching.

**Conclusion:**
The choice between AM and traditional methods depends on the specific application's requirements and budget constraints. While AM offers potential for future scalability and design freedom, it is currently more expensive due to higher material costs. Traditional methods are more suitable for immediate deployment but may be less cost-effective in terms of performance per unit.

**Key Performance Indicators:**
- **Bandwidth:** AM arrays can offer broader bandwidth compared to traditional methods.
- **Scanning Range:** Traditional methods generally provide better scanning range and impedance matching.
- **Cost Per Unit:** AM is more expensive, while traditional methods are cheaper but require higher overall costs due to material usage.

In summary, the choice between AM and traditional methods should be based on balancing performance requirements with financial constraints.
