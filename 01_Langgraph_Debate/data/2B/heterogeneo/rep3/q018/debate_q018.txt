[DLB] ### Frequency Planning Challenges When Coordinating Multiple Ground Stations with Overlapping Satellite Visibility Windows

When coordinating multiple ground stations with overlapping satellite visibility windows, several challenges arise:

- **Interference Between Satellites**: As satellites pass over the same geographic location, they can interfere with each other. This interference is particularly problematic for low Earth orbit (LEO) and geostationary orbit (GSO) satellites due to their fixed orbits. Interference can lead to reduced throughput and increased latency.

- **Resonant Frequencies**: Resonant frequencies occur when two or more satellites operate at the same frequency, causing significant interference. This is particularly problematic for LEO satellites that operate in close proximity to each other.

- **Resource Competition**: The shared use of radio spectrum between terrestrial wireless networks and satellite communications can lead to resource competition. Ensuring efficient management requires tight coordination among operators to avoid conflicts.

### Budget Implications

The budget implications of these challenges are significant due to the increased complexity and potential reduction in performance:

- **Reduced Throughput**: Interference and resonance issues can significantly reduce the overall throughput, leading to higher costs for data transmission.
  
- **Increased Latency**: Higher interference levels result in longer delay times, which are costly in terms of service quality.

- **Operator Coordination Costs**: Tight coordination among multiple ground stations requires significant resources such as personnel, equipment, and communication bandwidth. These operational overheads add to the overall cost of system implementation.

To mitigate these challenges, operators need to invest in advanced frequency planning techniques that minimize interference and maximize spectral efficiency. This includes using advanced algorithms for interference suppression, managing resource allocation effectively, and employing robust scheduling strategies to ensure smooth coordination among ground stations operating under overlapping satellite visibility windows.
