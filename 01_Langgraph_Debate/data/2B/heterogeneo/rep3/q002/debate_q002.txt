[DLB] The 25 GHz stopband in multi-beam satellite downlink systems introduces significant interference to adjacent beams, impacting both inter-beam interference (IBI) management and signal quality. The physical mechanism involves an array factor that distributes signals across multiple antennas, leading to overlapping coverage areas. This overlap can cause strong interference between neighboring beams due to the limited bandwidth of 25 GHz. To mitigate IBI while achieving high performance and coverage, phased-array antenna systems with single RF chains are employed. These systems use phase shifters to steer beams towards specific targets but cannot perfectly isolate beam patterns across adjacent beams due to spacing effects. The solution involves carefully designing the array factor such that neighboring beams do not overlap significantly in frequency space, thereby reducing IBI while maintaining high performance and coverage by optimizing signal distribution.

The budget implications of this design include:
1. **Signal Processing Cost**: Implementing phased-array antenna systems requires additional RF chain processing compared to traditional multi-beam satellite designs. This increases cost due to higher complexity and power requirements.
2. **Phase Shifter Costs**: The phase shifters used in the phased-array system are more expensive than simpler beam steering mechanisms like single-feed per beam (SFPB). These costs can be significant, especially for large systems with many beams.
3. **Interference Mitigation Complexity**: Managing IBI requires sophisticated algorithms and signal processing techniques, which increases development time and complexity. This adds to the overall cost of system integration.

By mitigating interference through phased-array antenna systems, high performance and coverage are maintained while minimizing additional costs associated with more complex beam steering mechanisms.
