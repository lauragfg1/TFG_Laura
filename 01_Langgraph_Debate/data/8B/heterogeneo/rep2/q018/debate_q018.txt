[PNM] In overlapping satellite visibility windows, coordinating multiple ground stations presents several challenges:

1. Interference between satellites on the same frequencies: If not managed properly, both terrestrial and satellite networks can interfere with each other, reducing capacity and spectral efficiency.
2. Crowded spectrum: The increasing number of satellites in LEO occupying amateur radio spectrum, combined with various modulation techniques, data rates, and protocols used by the cubesat community, demands efficient use of available spectrum through de-confliction or cognitive radio (CR) techniques.
3. Coordination to mitigate in-line events interference: Intersatellite interference can occur between an LEO satellite and a GSO satellite or between two close LEO satellites of different constellations operating on the same frequency. Tight coordination is essential to manage these interferences effectively, ideally by having the same operator managing both satellite and terrestrial networks.
4. Noisy and/or contested spectrum: SDR applications in recent years aim at handling multiple satellite signals at any given time, making maximum use of a priori information to minimize initial acquisition and detection bandwidths. Robustness is crucial in noisy and/or contested spectrum.
5. Phase noise impact on performance: Contention resolution slotted random access schemes can be affected by phase noise, causing degradation in system performance.
6. Frequency packing for interference alignment-based cognitive dual satellite systems: Efficient frequency packing is essential to minimize interferences between co-frequency, codirectional noneostationary satellite networks.
7. Short term interference statistics for circular orbits: Determining statistics of short-term interference between co-frequency, codirectional non-GEO fixed satellite service systems in circular orbits and other non-GEO fixed satellite service systems is crucial for efficient frequency planning.
