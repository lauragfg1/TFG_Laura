[PNM] During peak service demand periods, satellite beam switching can impact thermal/power constraints due to increased power allocation to hot beams to meet user demands or address adverse channels. Dynamic spectrum sharing techniques mitigate these effects by optimizing power allocation among multiple beams. For instance, game-theoretic approaches like joint pricing and power allocation allocate system resources based on demand, ensuring efficient use of available capacity while managing thermal/power constraints (Kan & Xu, 2015; Fiachetti et al., 2012).

Dynamic allocation schemes, such as Hunger Marketing, dynamically adjust resource allocation based on user priority, signal diversity, service types, and unstable weather (Zhao, 2016). These strategies ensure that more resources are allocated to hot beams or areas with higher demand, thus minimizing the impact of beam switching on thermal/power constraints.

In contrast, traditional approaches like overprovisioning network equipment may not cope with the increase in data traffic and can lead to resource bottlenecks (Colell et al., 2019). In this case, dynamic allocation schemes are more effective in balancing network efficiency against thermal/power constraints during peak service demand periods.
