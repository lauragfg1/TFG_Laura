[MODERATOR] **Technical Problem:**

Designing antennas for ground stations in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite constellations poses significant challenges due to short service windows and varying line-of-sight conditions. The goal is to optimize antenna design considerations to maximize visibility during these brief periods, ensuring reliable communication with satellites.

[DLB] To optimize ground station antennas for LEO constellations, focus on minimizing array factor to reduce interference from neighboring satellites. Optimal spacing between antennas should be balanced to avoid mutual blocking while maximizing visibility. For short service windows, a narrower beamwidth is crucial but increases the risk of shadowing and fading. Using SR (Shadowed-Rician) fading models helps predict these effects, impacting link budget and capacity. Trade-offs must balance these factors to ensure reliable communication with minimal performance degradation.

[PNM] Prioritizing key design considerations for ground station antennas in LEO constellations involves optimizing the trade-off between visibility, capacity, and complexity. Key considerations include:

1. Line-of-sight (LoS) Shadowed-Rician (SR) fading model to account for satellite visibility during brief service windows (Ω, b0, m).
2. Rayleigh fading power for the Gateway-User link and SR fading power for the S-GW link (W 2 s ; W 2 ).
3. SNR threshold at the user level (γu) and satellite level (γg).
4. Satellite, gateway, and ABS transmit power (ρs; ρg; ρa).
5. QoS-OSPF with RSVP for managing available capacity and providing detailed link status information (DVB-S2 [18], [113]).
6. Simplifying ground station equipment due to relatively small distances between satellites and ground stations in LEO constellations.
7. Rosette constellations for extending the coverage of LEO constellations without Inter Satellite Links (ISL).
