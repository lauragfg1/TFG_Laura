[PNM] Advanced modulation techniques like QAM (Quadrature Amplitude Modulation) and OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing) play a crucial role in balancing network efficiency against thermal/power constraints during peak service demand periods by optimizing spectral efficiency.

QAM and OFDM offer higher order modulation schemes, which increase the number of bits per symbol, thus improving spectral efficiency. This allows for more data to be transmitted within the same bandwidth, reducing the need for additional resources and minimizing thermal/power constraints. However, it's essential to ensure that the chosen modulation scheme matches the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) to maintain error-free communication and prevent overheating of components.

In addition, OFDM supports multiple carriers with orthogonal frequencies, enabling efficient use of available spectrum and reducing co-channel interference. This contributes to load balancing across different frequency bands and improves overall network efficiency during peak demand periods.

To summarize, advanced modulation techniques like QAM and OFDM help balance network efficiency against thermal/power constraints by increasing spectral efficiency, supporting higher order modulation schemes, and reducing co-channel interference through the use of multiple carriers with orthogonal frequencies.
