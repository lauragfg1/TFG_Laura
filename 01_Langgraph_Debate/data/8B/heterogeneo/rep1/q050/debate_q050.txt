[PNM] To ensure consistent LOS connectivity for A2A links in dynamically changing flight formations, adaptive beamforming strategies are crucial. These strategies should account for rapidly fluctuating frequency selective fading due to high aircraft velocities and Rician distributed fading when two aircraft fly in opposite directions.

Adaptive beamforming can be implemented using techniques such as Coordinated Multi-Point (CoMP) or Direct-Sequence Spread Spectrum (DSSS). CoMP allows multiple satellites to collaborate, improving signal quality by combining signals from different satellites at the receiver. DSSS spreads the signal over a wider bandwidth, reducing interference and improving robustness against fading.

Additionally, leveraging GPS for synchronization among network nodes can help schedule contention-free transmissions, enhancing stability in A2A communications. However, maintaining stable multihop connectivity remains a significant challenge due to the highly dynamic nature of high-speed aircraft and unpredictable mobility patterns. New routing protocols that cater to these specific requirements are needed for reliable data delivery in Aeronautical Ad-hoc Networks (AANETs).
