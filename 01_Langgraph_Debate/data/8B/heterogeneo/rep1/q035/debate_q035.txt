TOPIC: How can frequency planning address interference between satellite uplinks and broadband power line communication systems?
==================================================

[MODERATOR] **
------------------------------
[DLB] Frequency planning is crucial to avoid interference between satellite uplinks and broadband power line communication (PLC) systems. For instance, Ka-band frequencies used in satellite communications can overlap with PLC operating bands, leading to significant interference. The array factor from the PLC terminals can cause harmful interference within their main and side lobes, particularly affecting the uplink performance of satellites. To mitigate this, careful frequency spacing is essential, ensuring at least 30 MHz separation between critical bands. This spacing minimizes interference while optimizing spectral efficiency, though it may reduce overall system capacity by allocating more bandwidth to guard channels.
------------------------------
[PNM] Frequency planning can address interference between satellite uplinks and broadband power line communication systems by carefully selecting frequency bands for each system to minimize overlap. For instance, satellite systems typically operate in S, Ku, Ka, or L bands (2.65-2.69 GHz, 14.0-14.5 GHz, 27.5-31.0 GHz, 1.635-1.66 GHz respectively for uplink and 2.5-2.54 GHz, 12.5-12.75 GHz, 17.7-19.7 GHz, 1.535-1.56 GHz for downlink), whereas power line communication systems usually operate in lower frequency bands (<1 GHz). By avoiding overlap between these frequency bands, interference can be significantly reduced. Additionally, exclusion zones or isolation values can be set to minimize interference between satellite and terrestrial networks operating on the same frequency band.
