 In satellite communications, the selection of Phase Delay (PD) and Phase Rotation (PR) cells for Circular Polarization Transmit Arrays (CP TAs) impacts link budget margins due to their contrasting axial ratio and bandwidth properties.

PD cells offer a wider bandwidth and better axial ratio control, but are bound by the feed's axial ratio. This means that a higher-quality feed is required to achieve better axial ratio performance, potentially impacting link budget margins negatively due to the associated cost and complexity.

In contrast, PR cells have a narrower bandwidth but provide more flexibility in polarization switching, making them suitable for applications requiring polarization diversity and switching between Right-Hand Circular Polarization (RHCP) and Left-Hand Circular Polarization (LHCP), such as multicell satellite services. However, the filtering characteristic of PR TAs makes switching between RHCP and LHCP difficult, which can negatively affect link budget margins in certain applications.

Given these trade-offs, the choice between PD and PR cells for CP TAs should consider the specific project requirements, such as the need for polarization diversity, switching capabilities, and the quality of the feed available. This will ultimately impact the link budget margins and the overall performance of the satellite communication system.
