[PNM_Expert]:
 In defocused systems, the optimal feed element spacing balance that minimizes dB loss while maintaining beamforming flexibility lies at the intersection of reduced dB loss and increased flexibility. This balance can be achieved by striking a trade-off between the two.

On the one hand, increasing feed element spacing reduces dB loss by decreasing mutual coupling and interference among the elements. On the other hand, closer spacing enhances flexibility by providing a denser array capable of forming narrower and more directional beams.

To achieve the desired balance, one possible approach is to use a hybrid feed arrangement where the center feed elements are placed closer together to provide high gain and flexibility, while the border elements are spaced further apart to minimize dB loss and make the system more robust against feed failures.

Ultimately, the optimal spacing balance will depend on the specific system requirements, such as the desired coverage area, number of users, and the level of tolerance for dB loss and feed failures. It is essential to consider these factors when designing defocused systems to ensure optimal performance and reliability.
