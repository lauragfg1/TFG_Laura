   - **Advantages**: DPC can significantly improve the spectral efficiency by allowing the transmitter to account for the interference from other users. This can lead to higher capacity in multi-user MIMO systems.
   - **Complexity**: DPC is computationally intensive, making it challenging to implement in real-time systems. It requires solving a non-convex optimization problem, which can be computationally expensive.
   - **Interference Management**: DPC effectively manages interference by treating it as part of the channel state information, ensuring that the signal is transmitted in a way that minimizes interference to other users.

2. **Zero-Forcing Beamforming (ZF)**:
   - **Advantages**: ZF can provide near-optimal performance by nullifying the interference from other users, leading to improved reliability and reduced interference.
   - **Complexity**: ZF is less computationally intensive compared to DPC, making it more practical for real-time implementation. It can be implemented using simpler algorithms, such as least squares solutions.
   - **Interference Management**: ZF nulls out the interference from other users, but it may not always achieve the same level of spectral efficiency as DPC. However, it can still provide significant improvements in reliability and signal quality.

By balancing these factors, we can design beamforming strategies that ensure consistent LOS connectivity while minimizing interference and improving signal quality.
