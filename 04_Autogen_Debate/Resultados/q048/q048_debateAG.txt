 Addressing the conflicting requirements of aerodynamics and MIMO in high-speed aircraft communication antenna design can be approached by considering several strategies:

1. Compact antenna designs: Small, lightweight antennas are essential for aerodynamic efficiency. This can be achieved using microstrip patch arrays, phased arrays, or frequency-selective surfaces. These designs can provide sufficient gain while minimizing the form factor.

2. Beam-steering techniques: Beam-steering capabilities are crucial for maintaining communication links with other aircraft or ground stations. To balance aerodynamic constraints, adaptive beam-steering techniques, such as electronically steered phased arrays, can be employed. These arrays offer the advantage of electronically controlling the radiation patterns of the antenna, enabling beam steering without physically moving the antenna.

3. Material selection: Materials used for the antenna must satisfy the mechanical and thermal constraints of space missions and survive in the radiation environment of the selected orbit. Lightweight and radiation-hard materials, such as carbon fiber composites or ceramics, can be used to achieve both aerodynamic efficiency and radiation resistance.

4. Interference management: Interference between multiple antennas can be managed through techniques like spatial multiplexing, where each antenna element is used to transmit a unique signal, or space-time coding, where correlated interference is minimized by using specific signal processing algorithms.

5. Low Radar Cross-Section (RCS): To minimize the RCS, antennas can be designed with a low profile and integrated into the aircraft's structure. Additionally, the use of radar-absorbent materials (RAM) can help reduce the RCS further.

In conclusion, addressing the conflicting requirements of aerodynamics and MIMO in high-speed aircraft communication requires a multidisciplinary approach that considers antenna size, beam-steering techniques, material selection, interference management, and RCS reduction.

- **Radiation Resistance:** Materials like ceramics or specialized composites can provide excellent radiation resistance, ensuring the antenna can survive in the harsh radiation environment of high-altitude or space missions.
