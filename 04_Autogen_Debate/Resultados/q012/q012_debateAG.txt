[DLB_Expert]:
Load-aware beam hopping involves dynamically adjusting beam coverage based on real-time traffic load and user distribution. Here’s how it works:

1. **Traffic Monitoring and Classification**: Hierarchical traffic classification at levels 1 and 2 (L1 and L2) identifies different traffic classes and their QoS requirements. This classification helps in understanding the nature of the traffic.

2. **Load Distribution**: The system continuously monitors the network load. During periods of high congestion, priority is given to high-QoS traffic by allocating more beams to these users. Conversely, in less populated areas, lower-quality beams can be maintained, reducing unnecessary bandwidth usage.

3. **Adaptive Beam Switching**: The satellite network dynamically switches beams to ensure that resources are allocated efficiently. For instance, if an urban area is experiencing congestion, the network can allocate more beams to serve high-QoS applications like video streaming. Meanwhile, in rural areas, lower-quality beams can be used to maintain network efficiency.

4. **Avoiding Congested Links**: By continuously assessing the network state, the system can avoid directing traffic to congested links. This reduces queuing delays and packet drops, improving overall QoS.

5. **Trade-off Management**: The strategy balances the need for high-QoS traffic with the efficient use of lower-quality beams. High-QoS traffic is prioritized during peak times, while lower-quality beams are used in sparsely populated regions to minimize resource wastage.

In summary, load-aware beam hopping ensures that resources are allocated optimally based on current network conditions and traffic requirements, thereby enhancing overall network efficiency and QoS.
