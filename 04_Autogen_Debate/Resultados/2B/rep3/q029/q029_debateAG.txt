Theoretical peak gain is lower for larger apertures due to the increased loss, while measured peak gain is lower due to the practical limitations of the antenna.

On the other hand, redundancy in feed elements can mitigate these losses and improve the overall robustness of the antenna. By having multiple feed elements, the system can maintain performance even if individual elements fail. However, this redundancy comes at the cost of increased complexity and reduced gain for a given aperture size.

In the context of the dual-band circularly polarized fully reconfigurable reflectarray antenna, the trade-off is evident in the metrics provided. The maximum aperture directivity is higher for a larger aperture, but the actual gain is lower due to the increased losses. The theoretical peak gain is higher for a larger aperture, but the measured peak gain is lower due to practical limitations.

By employing a defocused reflector concept, the antenna is designed to be more robust against feed element failures. This involves reducing the aperture to a smaller size, which is offset by the increased redundancy in the feed elements. The smaller aperture results in lower unit cell loss, spillover loss, and taper loss, leading to better performance in terms of gain and robustness.

In summary, the trade-offs between reflector aperture size and feed element redundancy are significant, with a larger aperture providing better directivity but higher losses, and a smaller aperture with redundancy providing better performance but lower gain. The defocused reflector concept offers a balance between these trade-offs, improving robustness at the cost of reduced gain.
