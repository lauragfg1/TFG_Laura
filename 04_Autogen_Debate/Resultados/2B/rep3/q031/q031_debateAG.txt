### 1. Mechanism of MVDR Beamforming
MVDR (Minimum Variance Distortionless Response) beamforming compensates for pattern degradation by minimizing the distortion of the beam pattern. It achieves this by optimally combining the outputs of the feed elements to create a beam that is as close as possible to the desired pattern, while minimizing the energy spread. This is done by solving an optimization problem that considers the desired pattern and the actual pattern of the feed elements. The MVDR algorithm effectively compensates for the pattern degradation by ensuring that the beam pattern remains as close as possible to the desired pattern, even when some feed elements fail.

Compared to other beamforming techniques, MVDR is particularly effective in scenarios with significant pattern degradation. It is computationally efficient, making it suitable for real-time applications where rapid adaptation is crucial. However, it may not always be the best choice when the pattern degradation is minimal, as other techniques like Maximum Likelihood (ML) beamforming might be more effective.

### 2. Defocused Reflector Advantages
The defocused reflector design offers several advantages in terms of robustness against element failures. The key advantages are:
- **Reduced Gain Loss:** The defocused reflector generally has a slightly reduced gain loss after element failures, particularly in the center direction. This reduction is due to the reduced defocusing of the outer elements in the defocused reflector compared to the conventional reflector.
- **Better Performance in Border Failures:** The defocused reflector performs better in the direction of failed elements, which can be seen from the performance comparison in table II. This is because the outer elements in the defocused reflector show a reduced defocusing, leading to a better performance in the direction of failed elements.

### 3. Optimizing the Reflector Surface and Feed Array
Optimizing the reflector surface and feed array can be approached from several strategies:
- **Feed Array Geometry:** The feed array can be optimized by adjusting the spacing and orientation of the feed elements. For instance, bending the 2D feed array such that the outer elements come closer to the position of the outer feed elements of the conventional feed array can reduce the defocusing effect. This optimization can be effective in reducing the gain loss and improving the overall performance of the reflector.
- **Feed Array Configuration:** The configuration of the feed array can also be optimized by considering the failure scenario.
