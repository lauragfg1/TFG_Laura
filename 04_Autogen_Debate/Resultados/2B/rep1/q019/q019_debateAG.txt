Each satellite has a limited on-board memory buffer to store data packets of time-tagged commands. This constraint necessitates efficient use of available resources to balance between immediate and time-tagged services. 

### Storage Constraints and Power Allocation

Each satellite s has an on-board memory buffer of size bs, which can store data packets of time-tagged commands. At any time, the size of data packets uploaded on satellites s cannot exceed bs. This implies that the power allocation strategy must be carefully designed to ensure that the on-board storage constraints are met without compromising the performance of immediate services.

### Trade-offs between Fixed Allocation and Dynamic Allocation

For different service types, the trade-offs between fixed allocation and dynamic allocation can vary. Immediate services, which do not occupy on-board memory, are typically allocated power based on their demand. This ensures that these services are processed promptly without any delay. On the other hand, time-tagged services, which require additional storage on the on-board memory, must be allocated power based on the available capacity. This approach ensures that the maximum available capacity is utilized efficiently.

### Optimal Power Allocation

To optimize power allocation, it is crucial to balance the fixed allocated capacity to certain stations and the dynamically allocated capacity based on the traffic demands. This requires a dynamic power allocation strategy that considers the changing conditions of the instantaneous CSI and the changing traffic demands. By dynamically allocating power, the system can maximize the bandwidth utilization while adhering to QoS requirements, thereby minimizing latency and maximizing the overall system performance.

### Conclusion

Understanding the impact of on-board storage constraints on power allocation strategies is essential for designing efficient and reliable satellite communication systems. By carefully balancing fixed and dynamic allocation, the system can optimize power consumption and ensure optimal performance while meeting QoS requirements.

On-board storage constraints, particularly in low-orbit (LOO) and medium-orbit (MEO) satellites, are limited by their on-board memory capacities, which can store data up to 100 KB for LOO satellites and up to 100 MB for MEO satellites. The impact of these constraints extends to time-tagged services, which require additional memory space for processing after the satellite is ready.

The impact on latency and throughput is significant. Time-tagged services, which require additional memory space, often face increased latency due to the need for data storage and retrieval. This can lead to higher latency and reduced throughput. Additionally, the power allocation for time-tagged services must be optimized to balance processing time with memory usage, ensuring that the system remains efficient and responsive.

To address these challenges, advanced power allocation techniques are essential.
