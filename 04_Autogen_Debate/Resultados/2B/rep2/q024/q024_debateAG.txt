#### Performance and Capacity:
- **CTS Array Performance**: Traditional methods often rely on fabrication techniques that limit the achievable performance due to limitations in design freedom and production efficiency. In contrast, additive manufacturing (AM) allows for the realization of complex designs with fine tolerances, enabling the development of high-performance CTS arrays. The use of stereolithography (SLA) for the CTS array fabrication in this study results in a significant improvement in microwave performance compared to traditional methods.

- **Scalability**: AM offers scalability advantages by allowing the fabrication of monolithic designs, which can be scaled without increasing the complexity of the fabrication process. This is particularly beneficial for CTS arrays, which often require intricate internal structures. Traditional methods, such as CNC machining, are limited by the size and complexity of the parts they can produce, making them less scalable for large-scale satellite constellations.

#### Deployment Cost:
- **Initial Investment**: The initial investment in AM technology can be substantial, especially for the initial setup and training of personnel. This high cost is offset by the long-term savings in terms of reduced material and labor costs, as well as the potential for lower production costs due to the higher efficiency of AM processes. The cost per unit of production is significantly lower in AM compared to traditional methods.

- **Deployment Cost**: The cost of deployment is reduced due to the lower material and labor costs associated with AM. The lower cost per unit of production leads to a lower total cost of deployment, making AM a more cost-effective option for large-scale satellite constellations.

#### Interference and Design Choices:
- **Interference**: Traditional methods often result in higher interference due to the limited design freedom and the need for multiple components. AM, on the other hand, allows for the development of designs with minimal interference, as the fabrication process can be optimized to minimize the effects of surface roughness and other fabrication imperfections. This results in more efficient and effective CTS arrays.

- **Design Choices**: AM offers more design freedom compared to traditional methods, allowing for the development of monolithic designs that are difficult to achieve using conventional techniques. This leads to designs that are more compact, lighter, and more cost-effective, making them ideal for satellite constellations.

#### Conclusion:
The use of additive manufacturing (AM) for CTS array fabrication offers significant advantages in terms of performance, scalability, and deployment cost. The improved performance, combined with the scalability and cost-effectiveness of AM, make it a viable alternative for the fabrication of high-performance CTS arrays in satellite communications.
