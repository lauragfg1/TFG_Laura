In Q/V band feeder links, the gateway separation distance directly influences the spatial correlation and rain attenuation. As the distance \(d\) between gateways increases, the spatial correlation coefficient \(\rho\) decreases according to the formula \(\rho = 0.94 \exp(-d/30) + 0.06 \exp(-d/500)\). This reduction in spatial correlation leads to higher rain attenuation, which is modeled by the lognormal distribution. The increased rain attenuation translates into larger link budget requirements, specifically a 15-20 dB margin, to maintain the desired system availability.

The higher link budget margin necessitates more powerful transmitters or additional margin in the receiver design, both of which increase the overall system cost. Moreover, the link budget constraints can limit the achievable data rates and system reliability. To mitigate these effects, multiple gateway transmit diversity (MTD) and MIMO-based architectures can be employed. MTD allows for switching between gateways based on signal quality, ensuring higher availability, while MIMO can spatially separate the feeder links to reduce interference and improve performance.

In summary, increasing gateway separation distance degrades spatial correlation, leading to higher rain attenuation and stricter link budget margins. MTD and MIMO can help mitigate these effects, but careful system design is essential to balance performance and cost.
