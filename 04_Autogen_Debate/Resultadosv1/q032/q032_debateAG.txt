[DLB_Expert]:
In TDD satellite systems with large RTTs, RLNC's feedback mechanism becomes inefficient, increasing packet error rates (Pe) and bit error rates (Pebit). To mitigate this, optimizing antenna gain and coverage involves balancing between rapid feedback and reduced interference. Higher gain improves signal strength but increases interference, reducing effective coverage. Dividing transmissions into rounds reduces feedback overhead but limits throughput. Thus, the design must carefully trade off between gain, coverage, and feedback delay to minimize packet drop rates (PDR) while maintaining required delivery deadlines (Td).

2. **Interference and Coverage**: Higher antenna gain improves signal strength but increases interference, reducing effective coverage. This trade-off must be carefully managed to balance signal quality with interference.
3. **Throughput vs. Feedback Delay**: Dividing transmissions into rounds reduces feedback overhead but limits throughput. Each round introduces latency, which can be problematic for delay-sensitive applications.

**Trade-offs and Prioritization:**

- **Minimize Feedback Overhead**: Prioritize minimizing the number of transmission rounds to reduce feedback overhead and maintain low Pe and Pebit.
- **Optimize Antenna Gain**: Balance antenna gain to reduce interference while ensuring sufficient signal strength to meet required delivery deadlines (Td).
- **Throughput and Coverage**: Ensure that the design provides adequate throughput for delay-sensitive applications while maintaining coverage and minimizing PDR.
