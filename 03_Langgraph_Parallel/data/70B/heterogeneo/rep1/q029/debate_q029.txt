Larger apertures improve directivity but increase spillover loss (~5 dB for 2 dB taper) and taper loss (~1.35 dB). Redundancy via defocused feeds mitigates failure-induced gain drops by overlapping patterns, maintaining performance despite element failures. The budget impacts include higher losses for larger apertures and lower peak gains for redundancy, but improved robustness against feed failures.

1. **Aperture Size**: Increasing the aperture size enhances directivity, which is crucial for signal strength and directionality. However, this comes at the cost of increased spillover and taper losses. For instance, a 2 dB taper results in approximately 5 dB of spillover loss and 1.35 dB of taper loss, indicating significant energy inefficiency.

2. **Feed Element Redundancy**: Implementing redundancy through defocusing allows the system to maintain performance despite feed failures by leveraging overlapping patterns from adjacent elements. This enhances robustness but reduces peak gain, as seen in the drop from around 16.5 dBi to 14 dBi when using distributed energy.

3. **Mutual Coupling and Interference**: Closer element spacing increases mutual coupling, leading to higher interference. This complicates network management and reduces capacity. Advanced beamforming algorithms like MVDR can mitigate these issues by optimizing SINR dynamically, though they introduce additional system complexity.

In summary, the design choice between aperture size and redundancy involves balancing directivity and energy efficiency against robustness and interference management complexity.
