[DLB] To minimize signal attenuation in high-noise power line environments for reliable PLC integration, consider the following antenna design strategies and their performance implications:

1. **Impedance Matching**: Ensuring the antenna's impedance matches the power line reduces reflections, minimizing signal loss and attenuation. This enhances efficient power transfer but may require additional components or tuning.

2. **Directional Antennas**: Using directional antennas can mitigate noise by focusing signals away from interference sources like motors and fluorescent lights. While this reduces EMI, it may limit coverage area and increase system complexity.

3. **Adaptive Filtering**: Implementing adaptive filters to adjust for frequency-specific attenuation due to wave reflections can improve signal quality. However, this introduces potential trade-offs in latency and hardware complexity.

4. **Environment-Specific Optimization**: Designing antennas tailored to specific voltage levels or cable types improves reliability but may restrict flexibility across diverse environments.

5. **Physical Layout Considerations**: Antennas designed to account for power line topology can reduce propagation losses, though universal implementation may be challenging without detailed layout data.

Each strategy involves balancing performance aspects such as signal strength, interference management, and system complexity to optimize PLC reliability within the given constraints.
