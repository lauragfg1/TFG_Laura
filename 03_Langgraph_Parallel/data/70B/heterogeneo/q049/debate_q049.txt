[DLB] The integration of L-DACS1 and L-DACS2 into congested airspaces presents several challenges for spectrum sharing between A2G and satellite links, primarily due to physical mechanisms like interference, delay, and Doppler effects. 

**L-DACS1**, using Frequency Division Duplex (FDD), operates with simultaneous transmissions on different frequencies, which can lead to increased interference in shared spectrum bands with satellites. This necessitates careful frequency planning to avoid overlap, potentially reducing available bandwidth and thus affecting capacity.

**L-DACS2**, employing Time Division Duplex (TDD), alternates transmission directions on a single frequency, which may mitigate some interference issues but introduces synchronization challenges, especially over satellite links where signal delays are significant. The varying delays can disrupt the precise timing required for TDD operations.

Both systems face Doppler shift challenges due to aircraft movement, causing frequency shifts that degrade signal quality and require adaptive techniques to maintain link performance. Additionally, the long propagation delays in satellite communications can interfere with protocols expecting shorter latencies, complicating data synchronization and increasing error rates.

To address these issues, dynamic frequency allocation could minimize interference by adaptively selecting less congested bands. Implementing adaptive modulation schemes might help counteract Doppler effects by adjusting transmission parameters to current channel conditions. Scheduling data transmissions during periods of lower delay variability could also enhance performance without expanding the spectrum.

In summary, while L-DACS technologies offer advanced communication capabilities, their integration demands careful management of interference, delay compensation, and adaptive strategies to ensure efficient spectrum sharing with satellite links.
