[DLB] To mitigate interference between satellite uplinks and broadband power line communication systems, frequency planning should focus on minimizing spectral overlap while optimizing performance and capacity. 

1. **Frequency Separation**: Allocate distinct sub-bands to adjacent satellite beams to reduce interference from antenna side lobes. This approach, as noted in the context, helps mitigate intrasystem interference by ensuring that signals from different beams do not overlap, thus preserving the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR). However, this strategy may fragment the available spectrum, potentially reducing the total capacity of the system.

2. **Orthogonal Polarizations**: Employing orthogonal polarizations between beams adds another layer of separation, reducing interference without requiring additional spectrum. This method is particularly effective in high-throughput satellite systems, where polarization diversity can enhance overall system performance.

3. **Exclusion Zones**: Implementing exclusion zones where terrestrial users are limited in areas of high satellite traffic can reduce interference. However, this may constrain the density of terrestrial users, impacting the overall capacity of the terrestrial network.

These strategies involve trade-offs in terms of spectrum efficiency, system capacity, and interference mitigation, requiring careful balancing to ensure reliable communication.
