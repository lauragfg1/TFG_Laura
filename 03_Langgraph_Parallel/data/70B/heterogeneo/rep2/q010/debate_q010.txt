[DLB] In multi-beam satellite systems, interference mitigation techniques must adapt to tunneled versus non-tunneled traffic by balancing performance, capacity, and interference. 

1. **RSMA (Rate-Splitting Multiple Access)**: This technique dynamically allocates resources to manage inter-beam interference effectively. For tunneled traffic, which introduces overhead due to encapsulation, RSMA helps maintain throughput by minimizing interference without excessive resource allocation.

2. **ML/AI Techniques**: Machine learning and artificial intelligence optimize beamforming and power allocation by predicting traffic patterns and interference sources. This leads to efficient power usage and reduced interference but may require increased computational resources.

3. **Adaptive Beamforming**: Particularly useful for non-tunneled traffic, adaptive beamforming dynamically adjusts beam patterns to reduce interference from adjacent beams, maintaining signal quality without the need for encapsulation.

4. **Beam Hopping**: This technique involves rapidly switching beams to balance load and reduce interference hotspots. When integrated with RSMA or ML techniques, it enhances system robustness by combining dynamic allocation with intelligent resource management.

**Budget Implications**:
- **RSMA and ML/AI**: May require advanced hardware/software, increasing initial costs but optimizing operational efficiency.
- **Adaptive Beamforming**: Could increase energy consumption due to constant adjustments.
- **Beam Hopping**: Introduces complexity in beam management, potentially raising signaling overhead.

Overall, these techniques offer improved interference mitigation and performance optimization, with trade-offs in computational, power, and signaling resources.
