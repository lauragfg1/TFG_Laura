[DLB] The optimal antenna aperture size for balancing gain and portability in CMOS-based ground terminals for LEO and GEO satellites involves a trade-off between physical constraints and performance requirements.

For **LEO satellites**, smaller aperture sizes are favored due to their lower mass and higher portability, which is crucial given the need for frequent satellite tracking. However, smaller apertures result in lower gain, potentially impacting signal reliability under fading conditions like Shadowed-Rician models. The faster movement of LEO satellites necessitates agile antennas, possibly sacrificing some gain for the sake of mobility.

In contrast, **GEO satellites** can utilize larger aperture sizes to achieve higher gain without compromising portability, as they are typically stationary. This allows for better performance in terms of signal strength and reliability over their broader coverage areas.

The choice ultimately hinges on whether the application prioritizes mobility (favoring LEO with smaller antennas) or maximum performance (favoring GEO with larger antennas).
