[DLB] Prioritizing high-revenue services over navigation safety margins in dynamic scheduling frameworks can significantly impact the link budget through several physical mechanisms:

1. **Interference**: Allocating more bandwidth to high-revenue services increases the noise floor for navigation signals, potentially degrading their signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). This interference can stem from out-of-band emissions or adjacent channel leakage.

2. **Bandwidth Allocation**: Reducing bandwidth for navigation systems lowers their resistance to propagation losses and noise, as they have less capacity to maintain reliable communication. This can lead to dropped signals or degraded data integrity.

3. **Adaptive Coding Limitations**: While adaptive coding adjusts error correction strength based on link conditions, relying on lighter codes during favorable conditions may not provide sufficient robustness for navigation signals when interference spikes or channel conditions deteriorate unexpectedly.

4. **Array Factor and Beamforming**: If the system employs antenna arrays, increased traffic from high-revenue services can strain beamforming capabilities, reducing the effectiveness of null steering and beam formation aimed at minimizing interference for navigation signals.

5. **Latency and Jitter**: Congestion from prioritized traffic increases latency and jitter, critical issues for real-time navigation systems that require precise and timely data transmission.

In summary, prioritizing high-revenue services risks degrading navigation signal reliability through increased interference, reduced bandwidth, and potential failures in adaptive coding strategies, all of which directly impact the link budget's performance metrics.
