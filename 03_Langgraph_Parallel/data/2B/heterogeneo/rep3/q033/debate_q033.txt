------------------------------
[DLB] The key differences between GEO (Geostationary) and LEO (Low Earth Orbit) satellite orbits for rural substation monitoring networks are:

- **Latency**: 
  - GEO: Higher latency due to the large distance from earth, which can lead to delays in data transmission and processing.
  - LEO: Lower latency as satellites are closer to the ground, reducing communication time.

- **Coverage**:
  - GEO: Larger footprint but lower coverage efficiency per satellite.
  - LEO: Closer coverage with more efficient use of bandwidth due to reduced propagation delay.

- **Network Performance**:
  - GEO: Higher capacity and better throughput but at a higher cost due to longer delays and greater interference.
  - LEO: Lower latency, improved reliability, and higher data rates, which can lead to lower per-satellite costs but require more satellites for equivalent coverage.

- **Resource Efficiency**:
  - GEO: More expensive in terms of satellite count needed for the same coverage area due to longer delays and increased interference.
  - LEO: Cheaper as fewer satellites are required with reduced latency, potentially leading to better network performance per dollar spent.

In budget implications:

- **Latency**: Higher costs due to longer communication times can increase overall operational expenses. 
- **Coverage**: Lower cost due to more efficient use of bandwidth and lower delays.
- **Network Capacity**: Higher capacity but at a higher cost per satellite, which impacts the number of satellites needed for equivalent coverage.

The tradeoff is between latency reduction, network performance improvement, and increased satellite count costs versus reduced latency, improved reliability, and potentially better overall performance with fewer satellites.
