[DLB] **Antenna Design Impact on Classification Accuracy:**

In heterogeneous satellite networks, antenna design significantly influences encrypted traffic classification system performance through signal strength, interference, and beamforming. High-gain antennas can boost signal strength but may also amplify unwanted noise, affecting the clarity of signal reception for encrypted communications. Interference from other satellites or ground-based radio frequencies can distort classified packets, reducing accuracy.

Beamforming techniques optimize coverage by focusing signals in specific directions, enhancing directivity and minimizing interference. However, these designs require precise calibration to avoid blind spots where encrypted traffic might be misclassified due to weak or distorted signals. The choice of antenna array size impacts the ability to handle multiple simultaneous communications without overlapping beams, which can lead to confusion between encrypted and unencrypted traffic.

In summary, while high-gain antennas enhance signal strength for both encrypted and non-encrypted traffic, they introduce challenges in maintaining accurate classification by reducing interference margins. Beamforming designs require careful calibration to avoid misclassification issues, further complicating the optimization of encryption-aware systems within heterogeneous satellite networks.
