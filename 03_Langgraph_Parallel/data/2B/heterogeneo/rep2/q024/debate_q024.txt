[DLB] In additive manufacturing (AM) for CTS array fabrication, there are significant trade-offs between cost efficiency and scalability compared to traditional methods like CNC machining. 

**Cost Efficiency:**
- **Stereolithography (SLA):** SLA is more expensive than CNC due to higher material costs and labor-intensive processes.
- **CNC Machining:** Traditional techniques offer lower initial investment but are more costly per unit for larger production runs.

**Scalability:**
- **SLA:** Capable of producing complex designs with fine tolerances, making it suitable for high-performance CTS arrays. However, the higher cost makes it less scalable.
- **CNC Machining:** More affordable and scalable due to lower material costs per unit, but may not offer as much design flexibility.

In terms of deployment cost and scalability, traditional methods like CNC machining are generally more economical with lower initial investment and ease of scaling up production. However, AM offers the potential for higher performance at potentially lower overall costs by reducing size, weight, and production time while maintaining or improving certain key metrics such as bandwidth and scanning capabilities.

**Key Metrics:**
- **Performance:** AM can offer improved performance in terms of bandwidth, impedance matching, scanning range, and array efficiency.
- **Scalability:** CNC machining is more scalable with lower unit costs but may not match the design flexibility of AM for complex CTS arrays.
- **Cost Efficiency:** AM can lead to higher cost efficiencies due to reduced material usage and simplified manufacturing processes.

In summary, while traditional methods are generally cheaper in terms of initial investment and scalability, AM offers potential benefits in performance metrics that could justify a higher upfront cost. The choice should be based on the specific requirements of the application and the balance between design flexibility and production efficiency.
