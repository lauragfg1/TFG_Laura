[DLB] In defocused systems, minimizing signal loss (dB) while maintaining flexibility for beamforming is crucial for optimal performance. The key to achieving this balance lies in the feed element spacing and array geometry:

- **Feed Element Spacing**: Proper spacing between the feed elements minimizes signal leakage and interference. A smaller spacing reduces side lobes but increases directivity, leading to higher gain and lower loss. Conversely, larger spacings reduce directivity but increase side lobes, resulting in higher losses.

- **Array Geometry**: The arrangement of the array significantly affects both directivity (gain) and beamforming performance. An optimal geometry ensures that signals from different elements are well-separated, reducing interference while maintaining high gain for desired beams. This is particularly important in defocused systems where feed element failure can lead to reduced performance.

In summary, achieving a balance between minimizing signal loss and maintaining flexibility requires precise control over both the spacing of the feed elements and the geometry of the array. This ensures that the system remains robust against feed failures while optimizing for high gain and low losses in desired directions.
