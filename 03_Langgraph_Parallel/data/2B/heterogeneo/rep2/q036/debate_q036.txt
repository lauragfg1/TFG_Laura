[DLB] In satellite backhaul links, balancing data rate (throughput) with reliability under adverse weather conditions is challenging due to limited link budget constraints. The key trade-offs include:

- **Power Constraints**: High transmission power requirements for high modulation orders are necessary but may exceed the available capacity on satellites and terrestrial networks.
- **Latency**: Latency varies significantly between terrestrial networks and satellite links, complicating QoS assurance.
- **Interference**: Intermodulation products from multiple users can degrade signal quality, especially in congested satellite paths.

To optimize these trade-offs, consider:

1. **Modulation Techniques**: Use lower-order modulation schemes to reduce power requirements while maintaining acceptable data rates.
2. **Link Design**: Optimize link parameters such as antenna gains and spacing to maximize throughput without overburdening the link budget.
3. **Resource Management**: Implement adaptive resource allocation strategies that balance between maximum throughput and reliability under varying weather conditions.

By carefully balancing these factors, designers can create satellite backhaul links that provide reliable communication even in challenging environments while maximizing data rates.
