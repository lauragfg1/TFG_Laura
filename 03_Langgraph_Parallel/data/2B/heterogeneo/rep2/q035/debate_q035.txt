[DLB] Frequency planning strategies for mitigating interferences between satellite uplinks and broadband power line communication systems (PLC) can be implemented through careful frequency allocation:

1. **Avoid Overlapping Frequencies**: Ensure that no two systems operate on the same frequency band within the same geographical area to minimize interference.

2. **Use Frequency Spacing**: Implement a frequency spacing of at least 50 MHz between satellite uplinks and PLCs to reduce co-channel interference.

3. **Exclusion Zones**: Design exclusion zones around terrestrial users where their antennas' gain is below a certain threshold, effectively isolating them from the satellite's main beam.

4. **Dynamic Frequency Allocation**: Use adaptive frequency allocation techniques that can dynamically adjust frequencies based on system performance and environmental conditions to minimize interference.

5. **Channelization and Beamforming**: Employ channelizers and adaptive beamforming in both systems to reduce side lobe gains, thereby minimizing intermodulation products.

6. **Interference Mitigation Techniques**: Implement techniques such as pre-coder equalization, post-processing filters, or even software-based interference mitigation algorithms to enhance system performance.

7. **Coordination with Operators**: Ensure that operators manage their networks efficiently by sharing spectrum through coordinated efforts and possibly using exclusive access for certain types of traffic (e.g., critical services).

By implementing these strategies, the budget implications can be managed effectively without significant financial constraints, focusing on enhancing performance, capacity, and minimizing interference costs.
