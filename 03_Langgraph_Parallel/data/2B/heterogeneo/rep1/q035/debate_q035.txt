To minimize interference between satellite uplinks (2.65-2.69 GHz) and broadband power line communication systems (1.635-1.66 GHz), consider the following strategies:

1. **Spectral Spacing:** Ensure that the frequency bands used for both technologies are sufficiently spaced apart to avoid direct interference. The 1.635-1.66 GHz band is already occupied by broadband power line communication systems, so it's crucial to allocate a new, non-overlapping frequency range for satellite uplinks.

2. **Exclusion Zones:** Establish exclusion zones around the satellite antenna gain from the center of the beam. This reduces the impact of terrestrial users' interference on the satellite system.

3. **Channelization and Resource Management:** Utilize channel allocation methods to maximize traffic accommodation rates, ensuring efficient use of available frequency bandwidths without overlap.

4. **Weather-Induced Impairments:** Account for weather-induced impairments such as rain attenuation, which can significantly affect channel quality in high-frequency bands like Ka-band (27.5-31 GHz). Implement robust error correction and retransmission protocols to maintain data integrity.

By applying these strategies, the budget implications of design choices can be minimized through efficient resource management and careful consideration of interference effects on performance metrics such as signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), bit error rate (BER), and throughput.
