[DLB] ### Optimal Feed Element Spacing Balance

In defocused systems for satellite communications, maintaining beamforming flexibility while minimizing dB loss necessitates careful consideration of feed element spacing. The key challenge is balancing gain maximization with interference minimization.

**Gain Maximization:**
- **Array Factor:** Higher array factor (AF) at the border of the array results in stronger signal reception but also more defocused patterns.
- **Interference Minimization:** Spacing elements closer to the center reduces defocusing and improves overall pattern uniformity, thereby reducing interference.

**Flexibility with Feeds:**
- **Defocused Reflectors:** These reflectors benefit from reduced feed failures due to their robustness against individual or multiple feed element drops. The defocused concept provides a more consistent performance compared to conventional reflectors.
- **Feed Spacing Effects:** Optimal spacing ensures that the central elements receive maximum gain and the peripheral elements have slightly weaker signals but are less affected by feed failures.

### Balanced Design:

To optimize feed element spacing, consider:
1. **Central Element Focus:** Place the most critical elements (e.g., center of the array) at optimal distances to maximize gain without significantly reducing overall performance.
2. **Peripheral Feeds:** Optimize peripheral feeds to slightly reduce their contribution while ensuring minimal defocusing and interference.

### Performance Metrics:

- **Gain Maximization:** The central element should have a high AF, capturing most of the signal power.
- **Interference Minimization:** Spacing elements closer to the center reduces defocusing and minimizes interference from feed failures.
- **Flexibility with Feeds:** Ensure that feeds at the border are less affected by individual or multiple failure scenarios.

### Conclusion:

Balancing gain maximization, interference reduction, and flexibility in defocused systems requires careful consideration of feed element spacing. Optimal spacing ensures uniform pattern performance while maintaining robustness against feed failures. This balance minimizes dB loss and maintains beamforming flexibility, crucial for efficient satellite communication designs.
