[DLB] In tunneled traffic, the interference arises due to overlapping beams that have been re-routed through different paths from the same source (e.g., satellite base station). This results in non-simultaneous transmission of signals, leading to cross-talk and conflicting data streams. The physical mechanism involves array factors where each beam's signal is combined with others at the receiver, potentially causing strong interference if not mitigated.

In non-tunneled traffic, the main issue is direct line-of-sight (LoS) propagation between satellites and ground stations without re-routing through intermediate nodes. This results in potential inline interference when a geostationary satellite passes close to an Earth station on another orbiting satellite. The physical mechanism includes the coupling of beams that would otherwise be separated by significant distances, leading to strong interbeam interference.

Both scenarios introduce challenges related to signal overlap and spatial coherence. Tunneled traffic requires careful design of beamforming arrays to avoid array factors contributing to cross-talk, while non-tunneled traffic necessitates robust link budgeting and possibly switching mechanisms for inline events. Both types of traffic face performance penalties due to higher interference levels in the receiving end.

In terms of cost implications:
- **Design Choices:** The tunneled scenario requires more complex design to ensure beamforming arrays are optimized for non-simultaneous transmission, potentially leading to increased computational complexity and hardware requirements.
- **Link Budgeting:** Non-tunneled traffic necessitates robust link budgeting to account for the possibility of inline interference events. This may require additional margin in signal strength or bandwidth allocation, increasing link setup costs.

Thus, while both types of traffic present performance challenges, non-tunneled traffic introduces more immediate and significant cost implications due to the need for robust link design and potentially increased computational overhead in tunneled scenarios.
