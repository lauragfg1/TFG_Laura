Array factor, interference, and spacing effects are physical phenomena inherent in satellite systems that affect signal quality and latency:

- **Array Factor**: This effect amplifies the strength of signals received at specific angles due to multiple reflections off Earth’s surface. In satellite networks, this can lead to faster propagation times compared to terrestrial links.

- **Interference**: Satellite transmitters operate on a fixed frequency band with limited bandwidth, leading to increased interference and slower signal travel times.

- **Spacing Effects**: The separation between satellites in geostationary orbits results in additional time delays due to the curvature of space. This can cause higher latency compared to terrestrial links.

These physical mechanisms necessitate specialized path selection strategies that account for these unique characteristics.
