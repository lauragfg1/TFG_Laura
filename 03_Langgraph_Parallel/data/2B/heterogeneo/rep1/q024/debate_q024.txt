[DLB] ### Trade-offs Between Additive Manufacturing (AM) and Traditional Fabrication Methods for CTS Array Design

#### Performance vs. Cost:
- **Cost**: AM can initially appear cheaper due to lower material costs and labor savings, but higher setup times and complexity of design optimization may offset these benefits.
  
#### Scalability:
- **Scalability**: CNC machining is more scalable as it allows mass production of identical components with minimal errors. AM requires significant post-processing steps and customization for scalability.

#### Design Freedom:
- **Design Freedom**: AM offers greater freedom in designing complex shapes, reducing the need for multiple component assemblies.
  
#### Performance:
- **Interference**: Ameliorates interference issues by allowing finer control over material properties and geometry. CNC machining may introduce more surface roughness and imperfections that affect performance metrics.

#### Manufacturing Time:
- **Time Efficiency**: CNC machining is faster in terms of production time, especially for large-scale projects.
  
#### Material Selection:
- **Material Properties**: AM allows the selection of materials with specific electromagnetic properties tailored to the design requirements. Traditional methods may be limited by available materials and their physical constraints.
  
### Conclusion
In summary, while traditional fabrication methods offer better cost efficiency and scalability in terms of manufacturing times and mass production capabilities, AM provides unmatched flexibility in designing complex shapes and achieving superior performance metrics like reduced interference and enhanced bandwidth. The choice between the two should consider the specific requirements of the application and budget constraints for deployment costs and performance optimization.

This analysis highlights the trade-offs in both aspects—performance vs. cost and scalability—and underscores the benefits of additive manufacturing in terms of design freedom and material control, even at the expense of higher initial setup times and post-processing complexities.
