[MODERATOR] We're examining beamforming strategies to ensure consistent Line-Of-Sight (LOS) connectivity for Aircraft-to-Aircraft (A2A) communication in dynamic flight formations. This presents a unique challenge, as aircraft movements create fluctuating channel conditions and necessitate robust routing protocols. 

Let's explore the specific challenges of LOS connectivity, particularly considering the impact of velocity, position, and direction on signal propagation. How can beamforming strategies ensure reliable A2A links under these dynamic scenarios?  What are your thoughts on existing solutions like CoMP or DSSS, and what novel approaches might be needed to overcome this complexity?
------------------------------
[DLB] Beamforming for A2A LOS connectivity faces significant challenges due to aircraft velocity.  The rapid changes in direction and speed create substantial Doppler shift, leading to frequency-selective fading. This manifests as a distinct "rician distributed fading" pattern – the signal strength fluctuates rapidly between peaks and troughs. 

This dynamic channel environment demands adaptive beamforming strategies that can compensate for this changing signal propagation. The key lies in employing techniques like **Adaptive Beamforming** or **Multi-Antenna Systems**. These methods, while more complex, offer a higher degree of control over the signal path and mitigate the impact of Doppler shift, leading to improved reliability and capacity. 

However, implementing such advanced techniques incurs an increased cost due to complexity, requiring specialized hardware and software. This necessitates careful tradeoffs between performance, budget constraints, and practical implementation feasibility.
