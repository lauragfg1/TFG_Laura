TOPIC: What are the trade-offs in using additive manufacturing for CTS array fabrication versus traditional methods in terms of deployment cost and scalability?
==================================================

------------------------------
[DLB] In additive manufacturing (AM) for CTS array fabrication, finer resolution reduces surface roughness, minimizing RF losses but increasing initial setup costs. Traditional methods offer less design flexibility but lower upfront investment. AM’s higher material usage and complex part handling increase overall deployment cost, impacting budget more significantly than traditional methods.
------------------------------
[PNM] Using additive manufacturing (AM) for CTS array fabrication offers design freedom, compactness, mass, simplicity, cost, and production time advantages compared to traditional methods. However, trade-offs exist in terms of deployment cost and scalability:

1. Higher manufacturing cost: AM techniques like stereolithography (SLA) may introduce additional costs due to the need for specialized equipment and materials.
2. Lower scalability: Traditional manufacturing methods, such as Computer Numerical Control (CNC) machining, can produce multiple units more quickly and efficiently, making them more suitable for large-scale production.
3. Potential performance degradation: The surface roughness introduced by the AM process may affect microwave performance, leading to increased losses and potential degradation in antenna efficiency.
4. Limited material choices: While AM allows for complex designs, it may have fewer material options compared to traditional manufacturing methods, potentially impacting the overall performance and durability of the CTS array.
5. Longer lead times: The development and prototyping process with AM can take longer due to the need to optimize the design for the specific AM technique being used.
6. Quality control: Ensuring consistent quality across multiple units may be more challenging with AM, as each part is built layer by layer, potentially leading to inconsistencies in dimensions and material properties.
------------------------------
