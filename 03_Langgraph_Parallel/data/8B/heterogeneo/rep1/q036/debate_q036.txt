[PNM] In adverse weather conditions, satellite backhaul links face trade-offs between data rate and reliability. The link budget, which relates transmit to receive power, is crucial in understanding these trade-offs. For high data rates, modulation schemes with higher orders are used, but they require more power and are susceptible to interference from weather elements such as rain and clouds. On the other hand, using lower order modulation schemes improves reliability by reducing power requirements and interference vulnerability, but limits data rate. To balance these trade-offs, link budget calculations consider system parameters like antenna gain, frequency, and atmospheric conditions to determine an optimal power level for reliable transmission under specific weather conditions. However, this might still result in high latency due to the fixed nature of satellite links, which can negatively impact QoS.
