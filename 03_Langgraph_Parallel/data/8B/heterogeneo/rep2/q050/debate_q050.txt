This reduces unnecessary beam adjustments, minimizing interference and computational overhead. For instance, predicting that two aircraft will approach each other allows for pre-allocating overlapping beams to maintain consistent signal strength, reducing the need for rapid reconfiguration. However, accurate ATP models are crucial; inaccurate predictions can lead to suboptimal beam allocation, increasing interference and degrading performance. [PNM] Integrating Aircraft Trajectory Prediction (ATP) into adaptive beamforming can significantly enhance LOS connectivity for A2A links in dynamically changing flight formations. By anticipating the positions and velocities of aircraft, ATP can optimize the beam's array factor beforehand, reducing the need for real-time adjustments during flight. This proactive approach minimizes fluctuations due to frequency selective fading and Rician distributed fading, thereby improving overall system performance and capacity. However, accurate ATP requires precise GPS data and sophisticated algorithms to account for uncertainties in aircraft trajectories, which may introduce additional complexity and computational overhead.

To further improve the system's robustness against interference, beamforming strategies can incorporate Network Coding (NC) techniques. NC allows multiple beams to share a common frequency band by merging data streams from different users before transmission, reducing the need for separate beams for each user. However, this requires careful design of feed weights and decoding algorithms to ensure accurate signal reconstruction at the intended receiver(s).

In summary, integrating ATP into adaptive beamforming can improve LOS connectivity for A2A links by optimizing beam array factors proactively. Additionally, incorporating NC techniques can further enhance system capacity while managing interference.
