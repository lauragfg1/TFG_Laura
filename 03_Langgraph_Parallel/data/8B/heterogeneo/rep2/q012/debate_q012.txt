[DLB] Beam hopping strategies can optimize network efficiency by dynamically switching between satellite beams to balance load and improve coverage, reducing fixed latency impacts on QoS. However, frequent beam changes may introduce interference and increase overhead for link state detection, impacting performance. Interference management adds complexity but can be mitigated through careful beam design and spacing, reducing overall budget in terms of network reliability and efficiency.

[PNM] In satellite networks, hierarchical traffic classification is crucial for QoS management in converged satellite and terrestrial networks. QoS-OSPF with RSVP provides a solution by calculating shortest paths with sufficient bandwidth, blocking resources along the calculated path, detecting link status, and enabling admission control. However, it does not consider high fixed latencies on links negatively impacting QoS. ECMP approaches distribute traffic onto multiple links but are not based on QoS requirements. LTE relies on IP-routing for path selection in the backhaul segment, assuming a sufficiently provisioned network. DVB-S2 assumes all traffic is exchanged over the satellite link, with no path selection performed for the terrestrial link. SDN provides a flexible architecture for implementing functional building blocks but has limited monitoring capabilities on the southbound interface. To optimize network efficiency in beamhopping strategies, it's essential to consider these QoS-OSPF and related protocols for resource management while incorporating hierarchical traffic classification to account for varying link conditions and latencies in satellite networks.
