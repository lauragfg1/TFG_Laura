[MODERATOR] **Moderator's Introduction**

Welcome to today's technical debate on "Interference Mitigation Techniques for Overlapping Service Transmissions across Geographically Dispersed Ground Stations." With the increasing demand for seamless connectivity in underserved areas, satellite communication systems are being considered as a supplement to terrestrial wireless communications. However, overlapping service transmissions between ground stations can lead to significant interference, compromising system performance and reliability.

**Technical Problem Statement**

How can we effectively mitigate interference between overlapping service transmissions across geographically dispersed ground stations, ensuring reliable and efficient data transmission in diverse environments?

We invite experts from the SatCom/RF Engineering community to share their insights, experiences, and research findings on this critical issue.
------------------------------
[DLB] Interference mitigation in overlapping service transmissions requires careful array design. Using analog beamforming, we can reduce interference by optimizing antenna spacing to minimize spatial correlation (e.g., \(\rho_{tx} = 0.4\) in open rural environments). This reduces the array factor, decreasing unwanted signal components and improving overall system performance without significant budget impact beyond initial setup costs. However, this approach limits flexibility in rapid deployment scenarios.
------------------------------
[PNM] In geographically dispersed ground stations, overlapping service transmissions can result in significant interference, impacting system performance and reliability. To mitigate this issue, advanced techniques such as Rate Splitting Multiple Access (RSMA) can be employed for multigroup multicast and multibeam satellite systems [1]. RSMA splits the data into separate sub-streams and assigns them to different co-channel groups, thereby reducing interference [2]. Additionally, Adaptive Frequency Allocation (AFA) can be utilized to dynamically allocate frequencies to different cells based on the interference levels in an airport environment, improving overall system performance [3]. Furthermore, TDMA-based multiple access schemes can be implemented for aircraft communications, ensuring that each aircraft's packets are transmitted without causing mutual interference with terrestrial wireless communications [4]. However, it is essential to characterize the behavior of various sources of interference, such as multipath and cochannel interference, to maintain high integrity transmission in safety-critical aeronautical applications [5].
