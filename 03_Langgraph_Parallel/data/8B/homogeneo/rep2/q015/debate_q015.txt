The budget implication of this mechanism is a significant increase in inter-satellite interference (ISI), which can lead to decreased signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and reduced system performance. To mitigate ISI, more complex and power-hungry interference suppression techniques may be required, increasing the overall system's energy consumption and cost.

As satellites are placed closer together, their antenna patterns begin to interact, leading to changes in radiation patterns and increased interference. The spacing effects also affect the array factor, making it more challenging to maintain a stable communication link between satellites.
