[DLB] **Physical Mechanism of Co-Frequency Interference**

Co-frequency operation between satellite and terrestrial networks is plagued by interference due to their close proximity in frequency band. The physical mechanism behind this issue lies in the array factor of the antenna systems involved.

When two arrays are placed in close proximity, their radiation patterns overlap, causing constructive and destructive interference. This results in a significant increase in co-channel interference (CCI), particularly in the uplink scenario where the satellite receives signals from both its own users and terrestrial networks.

**Budget Implications**

The budget implications of this design choice are substantial:

1.  **Increased Interference**: CCI degrades the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) by 10-20 dB, leading to a significant reduction in system capacity.
2.  **Reduced Spectral Efficiency**: The increased interference requires a larger bandwidth allocation, reducing spectral efficiency and increasing the overall cost of ownership.

To mitigate these effects, advanced techniques such as analog beamforming and frequency planning must be employed to minimize CCI and optimize system performance.
