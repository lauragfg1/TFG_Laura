[MODERATOR] **Technical Problem:**

As we transition to extremely large aperture arrays, the "beam squint effect" becomes a significant challenge for frequency-selective beamforming strategies in SatCom systems. This phenomenon occurs when different frequencies produce beams with varying directions, compromising the array's ability to efficiently allocate resources and mitigate interference.

[DLB] **Beam Squint Effect: Physical Mechanism and Budget Implications**

The beam squint effect occurs due to the array factor's frequency-dependent phase response, causing different frequencies to produce beams with varying directions. This is exacerbated by the large aperture size of modern arrays, leading to a significant reduction in spatial bandwidth efficiency.

*   **Physical Mechanism:** The array factor's phase response can be approximated as a sinusoidal function of frequency and angle, resulting in a beam squint effect.
*   **Budget Implications:**
    *   Reduced spatial bandwidth efficiency (up to 50% loss)
    *   Increased interference between adjacent beams
    *   Decreased capacity due to reduced array gain

To mitigate the beam squint effect, designers can employ techniques such as:

*   Frequency-domain equalization
*   Adaptive beamforming
*   Array shaping and optimization
