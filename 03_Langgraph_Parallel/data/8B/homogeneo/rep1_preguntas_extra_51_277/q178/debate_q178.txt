[MODERATOR] **Technical Problem:**

In high-PER (Packet Error Rate) satellite channels with strict throughput-PDR (Packet Drop Rate) constraints, what are the link budget trade-offs between RLNC (Random Linear Network Coding) overhead and forward error correction?

This problem arises due to the inherent characteristics of satellite communications, such as long distance transmissions, high speed mobility, and harsh channel conditions. The goal is to optimize the trade-off between throughput and PDR while ensuring reliable data transmission.

**Experts, please share your insights on this critical technical challenge. How do you think RLNC overhead and forward error correction interact in high-PER satellite channels?
------------------------------
[DLB] **Trade-off between RLNC overhead and forward error correction:**

In high-PER satellite channels, increasing RLNC overhead to compensate for errors reduces throughput (η) due to increased packet size (q). However, this may lead to a decrease in PDR (Pd), as more packets are transmitted, increasing the likelihood of collisions and interference. Conversely, using forward error correction (FEC) increases the link budget by reducing the required transmission power, but may increase latency (Trt) due to retransmissions.

**Physical mechanism:**

The trade-off arises from the interplay between packet size, transmission power, and channel conditions. As RLNC overhead increases, packet size grows, increasing the array factor of the transmitted signal, making it more susceptible to interference and noise. FEC, on the other hand, adds redundancy to packets, increasing their size and reducing the required transmission power, but may introduce latency due to retransmissions.

**Budget implications:**

The optimal trade-off between RLNC overhead and FEC depends on the specific link budget constraints (e.g., throughput, PDR, latency). A higher RLNC overhead may be beneficial in high-PER channels with strict throughput constraints, while FEC may be more suitable for applications with tight latency requirements.
