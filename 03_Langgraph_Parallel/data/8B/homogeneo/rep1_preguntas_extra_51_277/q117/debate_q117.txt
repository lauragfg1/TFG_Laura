[DLB] **Aperture Size Trade-offs for Ground-to-GEO Links**

To balance beam spreading and signal strength, consider the aperture size trade-off between the optical terminal's transmitter and receiver. The transmitter's aperture size affects the beam divergence, while the receiver's aperture size impacts the collected power.

A larger transmitter aperture reduces beam divergence but increases the required receiver aperture size, leading to higher production costs and potential interference issues. Conversely, a smaller transmitter aperture minimizes the receiver's aperture size, reducing costs but increasing beam spreading and signal strength limitations.

**Optimal Aperture Size**

The optimal aperture size is determined by the trade-off between these opposing factors. A good starting point is to match the transmitter and receiver aperture sizes, ensuring a balance between beam divergence and collected power. However, this may not be feasible due to production cost constraints or interference considerations.

**Budget Implications**

The choice of aperture size has significant budget implications:

* Larger apertures increase production costs due to material requirements and manufacturing complexity.
* Smaller apertures reduce production costs but may compromise signal strength and data rate.

To minimize these costs, consider using smaller apertures with advanced beamforming techniques or adaptive optics to compensate for beam spreading. This approach enables the use of lower-cost materials while maintaining acceptable performance levels.

**Conclusion**

The optimal aperture size for ground-to-GEO links is a trade-off between minimizing beam spreading and maximizing signal strength. By carefully balancing these competing demands, it's possible to achieve an optimal design that balances production costs with performance requirements.
