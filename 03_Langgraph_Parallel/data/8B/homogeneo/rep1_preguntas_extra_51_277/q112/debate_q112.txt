[DLB] **Key differences in interference models between GEO and LEO systems:**

The physical mechanism driving inline interference is the array factor, which varies with satellite constellation geometry. In equatorial regions, LEO satellites' lower altitude and higher density lead to increased inter-satellite interference (ISI) due to reduced spacing effects. This contrasts with GEO satellites, where main beams are more aligned, reducing ISI but increasing earth station-generated interference.

**Budget implications:**

The design trade-off for LEO systems is between increased capacity and higher interference levels. To mitigate ISI, denser constellations may be deployed, but this increases the number of interfering signals, ultimately limiting system performance. In contrast, GEO systems prioritize reduced interference through main beam alignment, but at the cost of lower capacity due to fewer satellites in view.

**Dynamic/flexible approaches:**

Adaptive array processing and beamforming techniques can be employed to mitigate inline interference events in both LEO and GEO systems. These methods adjust antenna patterns in real-time to minimize interference from neighboring satellites or earth stations. However, their implementation requires significant computational resources and may introduce additional latency, impacting system performance.

**Impact on QoS:**

The increased complexity of adaptive processing techniques can lead to reduced QoS due to added latency and potential errors in interference mitigation. In contrast, static approaches like exclusion zones or coordinated frequency planning may provide more predictable QoS but are less effective against dynamic interference scenarios.
