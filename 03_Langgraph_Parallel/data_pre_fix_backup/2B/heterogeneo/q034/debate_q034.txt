[DLB] In high-noise power line environments, minimizing signal attenuation and distortion requires careful antenna design. The primary challenge is the high level of electromagnetic interference (EMI) from various sources, such as electrical motors, power supplies, fluorescent lights, and radio signals. To mitigate these issues, consider the following technical considerations:

1. **Array Factor**: The array factor of an antenna can significantly reduce signal attenuation by concentrating the signal in a narrower beam. This is particularly important in high-noise environments where signals need to be concentrated to minimize interference with other signals.

2. **Interference Mitigation**: Implementing techniques to reduce the impact of EMI, such as shielding and using directional antennas, can help minimize interference and improve signal quality. Additionally, using a phased array antenna can further enhance signal transmission by directing the signal in a specific direction, reducing the risk of interference.

3. **Spacing Effects**: Proper spacing between antennas is crucial to avoid mutual interference. This can be achieved through the use of directional antennas or by using multiple antennas with different polarizations to cover the same frequency band but with minimized interference.

By focusing on these physical mechanisms and their implications on signal quality, we can design a more reliable PLC communication system that operates effectively in high-noise environments.
