[DLB] The hybrid SIW-waveguide feeding topology in V-band satellite antennas addresses the challenge of balancing efficiency and compactness by mitigating insertion loss as array size increases. Here's how it works:

1. **Insertion Loss and Array Size**: As array size grows, the length of SIW lines increases, leading to higher insertion loss, which reduces efficiency. For example, moving from a 16x16 to a 32x32 array drops efficiency from 70% to 27.5%.

2. **Hybrid Topology Solution**: Replacing the SIW power divider with a hybrid SIW-waveguide power divider reduces insertion loss. Waveguides typically have lower loss than SIWs at these frequencies, thus maintaining efficiency.

3. **Trade-offs**: While the hybrid approach may slightly increase physical size, it remains compact. Smaller arrays (e.g., 8x8) don't benefit as much due to space constraints for waveguide integration, making 16x16 arrays optimal.

4. **Fabrication Compatibility**: The design uses standard multi-layer PCB and milling, ensuring manufacturability and cost-effectiveness.

5. **Performance Metrics**: The topology achieves a 14.6% reflection coefficient bandwidth and stable gain across the band, indicating good impedance matching and consistent performance.

In summary, the hybrid topology optimizes performance and compactness, crucial for high-efficiency V-band satellite antennas.
