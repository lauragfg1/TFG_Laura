[DLB] In dynamic scheduling, prioritizing high-revenue services can lead to a degraded link budget for navigation due to several factors:

1. **Effective Isotropic Radiated Power (EIRP):** Allocating more power to high-revenue services reduces the EIRP available for navigation signals, resulting in weaker received signals and higher Bit Error Rates (BER).

2. **Latency:** Prioritizing high-revenue services can cause delays in navigation signals, leading to outdated information and potential inaccuracies in real-time navigation data.

3. **Interference:** Increased power for revenue services may cause adjacent channel interference, degrading the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) for navigation signals and reducing reliability.

To mitigate these issues, consider:

- **Dynamic Allocation:** Maintain safety margins by ensuring navigation signals are prioritized regardless of revenue demands.
- **Frequency or Timeslot Separation:** Use different frequencies or timeslots to minimize interference between services.
- **Adaptive Power Control:** Adjust power levels dynamically to prioritize safety without compromising revenue service performance.

These strategies ensure a balanced link budget that maintains reliability for both high-revenue services and critical navigation systems.
