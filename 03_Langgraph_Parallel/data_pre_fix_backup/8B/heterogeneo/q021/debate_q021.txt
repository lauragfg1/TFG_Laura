[DLB] Transponder allocation must balance thermal and power constraints with network efficiency. During peak demand, allocating more transponders to hot spots reduces overall interference but increases power consumption. This trade-off impacts performance and capacity. For instance, focusing on high-traffic beams can improve user experience but may overload those transponders, leading to reduced reliability and increased thermal stress. Thus, dynamic allocation strategies are crucial, balancing these costs to maintain optimal network efficiency.

More bandwidth is allocated to hot zones (cells with high traffic demand) to meet user demands and address adverse channels.

Dynamic spectrum sharing and market-based techniques have been introduced to enhance energy efficiency in MSS. These methods allow for the allocation of more system resources to hot beams, improving network efficiency while managing power constraints. However, the interference temperature constraint in cognitive satellite communications can saturate the outage probability of the system, reducing system diversity. As the interference temperature constraint becomes less stringent, the outage error floor reduces, and the outage performance of the system improves.
