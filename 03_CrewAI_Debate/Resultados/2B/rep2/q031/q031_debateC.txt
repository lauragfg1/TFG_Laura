[Round 2] ### MVDR Beamforming Mechanism and Performance

MVDR beamforming mitigates pattern degradation caused by element failures in defocused reflector designs by compensating for the reduced gain in the border elements. This is achieved through:

1. **Mathematical Underpinnings:**
   MVDR (Minimum Variance Distortionless Response) beamforming uses the concept of minimum mean squared error to maximize the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR). It optimizes the weights of the antenna elements to minimize the distortion of the beam pattern, thereby compensating for the reduced gain in the border elements.

2. **Border Element Performance:**
   Border elements exhibit less defocusing, leading to improved performance compared to center elements even after failures. This is due to the reduced defocusing effect on border elements, which helps maintain the overall gain structure of the reflector.

3. **Conventional Reflector Weakness:**
   Conventional reflectors are highly susceptible to failures, requiring redundancy or margin in the SNR design. This makes them less robust and less efficient in terms of signal recovery and network performance.

### Performance Comparison

In various failure scenarios, the impact of feed failures varies:

- **Zero-degree Direction:**
  The gain loss is 1.52 dB for the defocused reflector, which is slightly worse than the conventional reflector but still significantly better than the loss in the direction of the eighth element.

- **3.56° Direction:**
  The gain loss is 1.70 dB, which is also worse than the conventional reflector but still much better than the loss in the direction of the fifth element.

### Cost in Terms of Performance and Capacity

- **Performance Improvement:**
  MVDR beamforming compensates for the reduced gain in the border elements, leading to better overall performance. This improvement is particularly significant in terms of network efficiency and signal recovery, enhancing the robustness of the communication system.

- **Capacity Considerations:**
  The cost in terms of capacity is the increased complexity and potential need for additional feed elements or redundancy in the feed array. However, the improved performance of the defocused reflector outweighs these costs in most scenarios.

### Summary

MVDR beamforming significantly improves the performance of defocused reflector designs by compensating for the reduced gain in the border elements. This leads to better network efficiency and signal recovery, enhancing the overall robustness of the communication system, even in the presence of feed failures.
