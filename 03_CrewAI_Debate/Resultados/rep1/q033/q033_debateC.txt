[Round 1] In hybrid networks for rural substation monitoring, LEO satellites offer lower latency and better coverage due to their closer proximity to Earth. However, this comes with array factor and interference challenges. The closer spacing of LEO satellites (500-2000 km) increases the likelihood of mutual interference, especially in densely packed constellations. This can degrade link quality and reduce capacity. Additionally, the array factor, which is the constructive and destructive interference pattern created by multiple satellite antennas, can further complicate signal reception, necessitating more sophisticated beamforming techniques. These design choices come at the cost of increased complexity and potential performance trade-offs.

[Round 2] In LEO constellations, the closer spacing of satellites increases mutual interference, impacting link quality and capacity. The array factor, due to multiple satellite antennas, creates constructive and destructive interference, complicating signal reception. To mitigate this, advanced beamforming techniques are required, adding complexity. GEO satellites, while offering higher latency, reduce interference and simplify network design, though coverage is less extensive. This highlights the trade-offs in choosing between LEO and GEO orbits for rural substation monitoring, emphasizing the need for sophisticated payload management and network architecture.

GEO satellites, despite higher latency, reduce interference and simplify network design but offer less extensive coverage. This highlights that LEO’s closer proximity provides better coverage but at the cost of increased complexity in managing mutual interference, while GEO’s broader coverage requires simpler but less frequent management strategies.

[Round 4] In LEO constellations, the closer proximity of satellites leads to increased mutual interference, which degrades link budget and capacity. This highlights the need for tailored network and payload management strategies in hybrid networks, balancing coverage and complexity effectively.
