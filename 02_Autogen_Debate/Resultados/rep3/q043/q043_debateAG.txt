1. **Reduced Interference**: Higher frequencies in V-band minimize interference from other satellites and ground-based sources, which is crucial in densely packed LEO constellations.
2. **Lower Array Factor**: The higher frequency reduces the array factor, leading to better spatial resolution and reduced interference from other satellites.
3. **Lower Atmospheric Absorption**: While V-band does suffer from higher atmospheric absorption, it is still more manageable than the higher absorption in optical bands, especially at higher altitudes.

For optimal performance, constellations with a large number of satellites in close orbit (e.g., tens to hundreds of satellites) benefit most from V-band. The close proximity of satellites in these constellations reduces the impact of atmospheric absorption and minimizes interference, making V-band a more viable choice.

In contrast, optical systems are limited by atmospheric conditions and require precise alignment, which can be challenging in the dynamic environment of LEO constellations.
