**Moderator's Question:** How do the different transmission techniques in L-DACS1 (FDD) and L-DACS2 (TDD) affect the spectrum sharing between A2G and satellite links, and what are the implications for interference mitigation and coordination in congested airspaces?

**Analysis:**

1. **FDD in L-DACS1:**
   - **Frequency Spacing:** FDD involves simultaneous transmission on different frequency bands between ground stations (GS) and airborne equipment. This can lead to more frequent and complex interference scenarios, especially in congested areas where multiple A2G and satellite links operate.
   - **Interference Mitigation:** Proper frequency planning and guard bands are crucial to minimize interference. However, in high-density areas, managing these guard bands becomes challenging, potentially leading to increased interference.
   - **Coordination:** Tight coordination between A2G and satellite operators is essential to avoid overlap and ensure smooth operation.

2. **TDD in L-DACS2:**
   - **Time Division:** TDD uses the same frequency band for both uplink and downlink transmissions in distinct time intervals. This can reduce the need for guard bands but introduces more stringent timing requirements.
   - **Interference Mitigation:** The inter-symbol interference (ISI) and inter-carrier interference (ICI) can be more pronounced due to the shared frequency band. Advanced beamforming and spatial diversity techniques can help mitigate these issues.
   - **Coordination:** Synchronized timing between the ground and airborne equipment is critical. Any deviation can lead to significant interference, impacting both A2G and satellite links.

**Implications:**
- **Performance Cost:** Both FDD and TDD techniques can lead to reduced spectral efficiency and increased interference, particularly in high-density airspace.
- **Interference Management:** Effective interference mitigation strategies, such as dynamic spectrum access and advanced beamforming, are necessary to manage the shared spectrum.
- **Operational Coordination:** Coordinated management by network operators is essential to ensure seamless operation and avoid conflicts.

By understanding these technical aspects, we can better address the challenges of integrating L-DACS into congested airspaces and optimize spectrum sharing.
