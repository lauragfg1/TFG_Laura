One approach is to increase the transmission power of the satellite link, which can help mitigate the effects of adverse weather conditions. However, this comes at a cost of increased energy consumption and potential interference with other satellite systems. Another approach is to improve the link characteristics, such as by using higher modulation orders or advanced error correction techniques, to compensate for the limitations of the link budget. 

It is also important to consider the different latency characteristics of satellite links, which can be significantly higher than those of terrestrial networks. This can lead to increased jitter and latency, which can negatively impact the performance of applications that rely on low latency and jitter-free communication. To mitigate these limitations, designers must ensure that the link is capable of providing QoS guarantees, such as ensuring that the link can be closed when necessary. 

In summary, balancing data rate and reliability in adverse weather conditions requires careful consideration of the link budget and trade-offs between different parameters. By increasing transmission power, improving link characteristics, and ensuring QoS guarantees, designers can mitigate the limitations of satellite backhaul links and provide reliable communication in adverse weather conditions.
