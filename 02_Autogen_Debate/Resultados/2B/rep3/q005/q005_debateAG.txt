The challenge of maintaining high data rates while reducing the cost through efficient payload design has motivated the quest for spectrally efficient transmissions. In the context of high-power amplifiers (HPAs) used in satellites, nonlinear distortions are a significant issue, leading to severe interference effects such as ISI and adjacent channel interference (ACI). The performance of the transponder is further compromised by the non-linear characteristic of the amplifier combined with the memory effects of channelization filters, which generates ISI. Reducing the on-board amplifier power efficiency not only increases the equivalent cost per bit but also limits the transmission rate due to a reduction in the received signal to noise ratio. In the literature, methods proposed to compensate for nonlinear interference can be divided into two categories: equalization and pre-distortion. Equalization compensates for the nonlinear effects through a feedback loop to restore the signal to its original form, while pre-distortion reduces the nonlinear effects by compensating for them before they occur. This approach aims to achieve a high spectral and power efficiency with acceptable performance.

To improve power efficiency in dense payload configurations, one potential solution is to use pre-distortion to compensate for the nonlinear effects of the amplifier. Pre-distortion involves modifying the input signal before it reaches the amplifier to reduce the nonlinear effects and improve the overall signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). This can be achieved through the use of adaptive equalization algorithms that adjust the signal in real-time to minimize the nonlinear distortions.

Another potential solution is to reduce the number of inner points in the constellation, which are transmitted at a lower power, thereby improving the dc efficiency of the HPA. This approach allows for a better trade-off between the outer ring points, which represent a good balance between error performance and resilience to nonlinearity, and the inner points, which are transmitted at a lower power and correspond to a lower dc efficiency.

In summary, pre-distortion and reducing the number of inner points in the constellation are potential strategies for optimizing power efficiency in dense payload configurations while mitigating ISI and ACI. These approaches aim to balance the trade-offs between error performance and dc efficiency, ultimately leading to improved power efficiency while maintaining acceptable performance.
