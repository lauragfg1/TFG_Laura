In hybrid satellite-WiMAX networks, optimizing spectrum efficiency during peak demand is paramount. The primary challenge lies in balancing the heterogeneous traffic demands across multiple beams, while minimizing interference and maximizing utilization of available bandwidth. 

### Beam Allocation Techniques
One effective strategy involves adaptive beam allocation based on traffic density and interference patterns. For instance, in a time division multiplex (TDM) satellite system, rational time slot and transmit power resource allocation is crucial. Dynamic spectrum access and sharing between satellite and terrestrial networks are key to managing idle satellite spectrum efficiently.

### Impact of Traffic Demand
As traffic demands fluctuate, beam allocation strategies must adapt dynamically. For example, when a cell experiences high traffic, more bandwidth can be focused on that cell, thereby enhancing performance. Conversely, during low traffic periods, beams with lower demand can be allocated to other cells, reducing spectrum waste.

### Dynamic Spectrum Access and Sharing
Dynamic spectrum access and sharing between satellite and terrestrial networks is essential for efficient spectrum management. Cognitive satellite communications can share satellite spectrum with terrestrial networks, enhancing the utilization of idle satellite spectrum. This approach leverages the flexibility of satellite systems to optimize resource allocation and improve overall network performance.

By focusing on these technical aspects, we can optimize spectrum efficiency in hybrid satellite-WiMAX networks, ensuring peak demand is met efficiently while minimizing interference and maximizing resource utilization.
