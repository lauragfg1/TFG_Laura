- **Latency:** Long delays due to their large, stationary footprint, making them suitable for global coverage but inefficient for immediate data needs.
- **Coverage:** Broad coverage with low resolution, ideal for rural and remote areas where high latency is acceptable.
- **Reliability:** High reliability due to their stationary nature, but limited flexibility and poor reliability in dynamic environments.
- **Cost:** High costs due to the need for large ground stations and expensive satellite systems.

#### LEO Satellites:
- **Latency:** Lower latency, ideal for immediate data needs, but limited global coverage due to their lower altitude.
- **Coverage:** Broad coverage with high resolution, suitable for dense urban areas and dense substation monitoring.
- **Reliability:** Reliability is improved due to their lower altitude, but still faces challenges with reliability in dynamic environments.
- **Cost:** Lower costs due to smaller ground stations and lower satellite costs.

- **Latency:** A hybrid approach combines the strengths of both GEO and LEO, offering a balance between latency and coverage.
- **Coverage:** A hybrid network can provide global coverage with enhanced data rates, making it suitable for rural substation monitoring.
- **Reliability:** A hybrid network can improve reliability by leveraging the high reliability of LEO satellites, complementing the low latency of GEO satellites.
- **Cost:** A hybrid network can offer a cost-effective solution by combining the higher reliability of LEO satellites with the lower costs of GEO satellites.
