The coverage area of a phased array antenna is determined by the arrangement and spacing of its elements. Grating lobes are unwanted signal peaks that can degrade the performance of satellite links. They are particularly problematic in Ka-band communications due to the high frequency and the need for high directivity. Interleaved array element spacing can help suppress grating lobes by reducing the number of elements needed to achieve a given gain, thereby minimizing the formation of unwanted lobes.

The choice of element spacing in an interleaved array plays a crucial role in determining the coverage patterns and grating lobe suppression in Ka-band satellite links.
