This question focuses on a critical aspect of AM-fabricated CTS arrays. Surface roughness can significantly impact the electromagnetic performance of CTS arrays, leading to increased losses and potential degradation of the array's overall performance. Mitigating these challenges requires a detailed understanding of the fabrication process and the properties of the materials used.

Key challenges include:
1. **Surface Finish Quality**: AM processes, such as stereolithography, often result in surface roughness that can introduce additional losses and affect the phase and amplitude accuracy of the array elements.
2. **Material Properties**: The choice of photopolymer resin and the post-processing steps, such as copper plating, can influence the final surface finish and overall performance.

Mitigation strategies could involve:
1. **Post-Processing Techniques**: Implementing surface smoothing techniques, such as chemical polishing or additional finishing steps, to reduce roughness.
2. **Material Selection**: Choosing resins with better surface finish properties or optimizing the printing parameters to achieve smoother surfaces.
3. **Validation and Characterization**: Conducting thorough characterization and validation of the fabricated arrays to ensure they meet the required performance metrics.

By addressing these challenges, we can optimize the performance and cost-effectiveness of AM-fabricated CTS arrays in satellite communication systems.
