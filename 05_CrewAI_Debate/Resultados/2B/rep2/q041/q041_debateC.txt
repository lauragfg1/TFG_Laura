The trade-off between array size and radiation efficiency can be quantified by examining the relationship between the array's dimensions and its radiation pattern. [Round 2] **New Insight:**

The specific implementation of SIW (e.g., waveguide vs. patch) significantly impacts the relationship between array size and efficiency. A waveguide configuration is generally more efficient in terms of radiation efficiency due to its ability to concentrate power in a specific direction, whereas a patch configuration may suffer from higher insertion loss due to increased power division and spacing. This highlights the importance of choosing the right implementation for optimal performance and efficiency.

Practical constraints such as spacecraft size and power budget play a crucial role in determining the optimal array size. Larger arrays may require more power to maintain high efficiency, thus limiting the practicality of larger designs. Conversely, smaller arrays can be more compact and less power-intensive, but may sacrifice some performance. The optimal array size must balance these factors to achieve the best performance and practicality for a given application.

The antenna's design, particularly the feed network and beamforming, significantly impacts the trade-off between efficiency and complexity. A well-designed feed network can mitigate some of the losses associated with larger arrays, thereby enhancing overall performance. Additionally, advanced beamforming techniques can further optimize the array's performance by steering the beam more precisely and efficiently, reducing interference and improving efficiency. This suggests that the choice of design elements, such as the feed network and beamforming, is crucial in minimizing the impact of trade-offs between efficiency and complexity.
