To achieve this, consider the following physical and budget implications:

1. **Array Factor and Interference**: The array factor of a satellite dish determines the coverage area and the number of beams that can be used effectively. Higher array factor means more beams can be used, but it also increases interference between beams. Therefore, selecting the optimal array factor is critical to balance coverage and interference. For example, a higher array factor can lead to better coverage but increases the likelihood of interference between beams.

2. **Spacing Effects**: The spacing between beams can significantly affect the network performance. Closer spacing reduces interference but increases the risk of signal overlap, leading to decreased capacity. Conversely, wider spacing increases capacity but at the cost of increased interference. The optimal spacing should be carefully chosen to maximize efficiency while minimizing interference.

3. **Power Consumption**: Adaptive power allocation is essential to balance network capacity and power consumption. Higher power allocation can lead to increased capacity but also higher power consumption, which can be a limiting factor in terms of energy efficiency and cost. Therefore, power allocation should be optimized to match the traffic demand while minimizing power consumption.

4. **Spectral Efficiency**: The allocation of spectrum bandwidth is critical, as it directly impacts the capacity of the network. Allocating more bandwidth to cells with higher traffic demand can help maximize network performance. However, this comes at the cost of higher power consumption, which needs to be balanced with the need for increased capacity.

5. **Inter-cell Interference**: Inter-cell interference is a significant challenge in hybrid satellite-WiMAX networks. Effective spectrum reuse and dynamic spectrum access strategies can help reduce interference, leading to improved network performance. However, these strategies require careful design to balance the need for efficient spectrum utilization with the need to manage interference.

By leveraging these physical and budget implications, beam allocation strategies can be optimized to maximize network capacity and minimize power consumption, thereby achieving optimal spectrum utilization and network performance during peak demand.
