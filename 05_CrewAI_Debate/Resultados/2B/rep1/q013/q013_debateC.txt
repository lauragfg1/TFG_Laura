The channel gain, influenced by factors like path loss and fading, also plays a crucial role in determining the SNR. In different orbital configurations, such as Low Earth Orbit (LEO), the noise performance is influenced by the receiver's noise figure (NF) and system noise temperature (Tsys). The SNR is further affected by the fading models of the propagation channel, including Line-of-Sight (LOS), Non-Linear Fading (NLF), and Scattered (S) components. The SNR is given by the ratio of the signal power to the noise power, and it is directly impacted by the path loss and fading components.

The array factor of the satellite and the interference from multiple satellites can also impact the SNR. The array factor represents the gain of the satellite's antenna pattern, which is influenced by the number and orientation of the satellite's feeds. Interference from other satellites can degrade the SNR by introducing additional noise components. The spacing effects, such as the distance between the satellite and the user, also influence the SNR by affecting the signal path and the noise environment.

In diverse orbital configurations, such as LEO, the trade-offs between dynamic range and noise performance are more pronounced. The noise performance in LEO is influenced by the system's noise characteristics, which can be affected by factors like the receiver's noise figure and the system noise temperature.
