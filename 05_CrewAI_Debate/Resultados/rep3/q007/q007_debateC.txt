To optimize QoS, we must consider the array factor, which affects the effective gain and thus the link budget. Closer satellite spacing can reduce array factor, but this comes at the cost of increased interference. Additionally, the spacing between satellites influences the overall system's ability to handle multiple beams, impacting capacity. A tighter spacing can increase capacity but may also introduce more interference, affecting performance. Therefore, an optimal balance must be struck to minimize latency and interference while maximizing capacity. This analysis reveals the trade-offs inherent in satellite orbital configurations for QoS optimization.

[Round 2] In optimizing satellite orbital configurations for ML-based QoS, closer satellite spacing can reduce the array factor, improving effective gain and thus reducing propagation delays. Therefore, an optimal balance must be struck, leveraging ML to minimize latency and interference while maximizing capacity. This insight highlights the necessity of integrating ML into satellite network design to achieve QoS optimization for latency-sensitive applications.
