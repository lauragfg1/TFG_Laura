For instance, closer beam spacing reduces spatial isolation, leading to higher intercell interference (ICI). This can degrade performance and capacity. Specifically, each additional beam increases the possibility of ICI, impacting link budget calculations. For example, if the array factor of a beam is 10 dB and the spacing between beams is reduced from 5 degrees to 3 degrees, the ICI could increase by up to 3 dB, reducing the effective signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and thereby the overall capacity. Thus, balancing the number of beams and spacing is essential for optimizing spectrum efficiency while minimizing intercell interference.

This insight highlights the trade-off between increasing coverage with more beams and managing the associated interference, which directly impacts the network's performance and capacity during peak demand.
